## 1. The Model

Lencioni's model is a **pyramid of five sequential dysfunctions**. Each level builds on the one below it. Fix from the **bottom up** -- strengthening foundations before tackling higher-level problems. A dysfunction at one level breeds underperformance at the next, like links in a chain (slide 9 visual: chain image).

```
            /\
           /  \
          / 5. \          Inattention to RESULTS
         /------\            "Status and Ego"
        /   4.   \        Avoidance of ACCOUNTABILITY
       /----------\          "Low Standards"
      /     3.     \      Lack of COMMITMENT
     /--------------\        "Ambiguity"
    /      2.       \    Fear of CONFLICT
   /------------------\      "Artificial Harmony"
  /        1.          \ Absence of TRUST
 /----------------------\    "Invulnerability"
```

Each level of the pyramid has a **root cause label** shown on the slides:

| Level | Dysfunction | Root Cause (slide label) |
|-------|-------------|--------------------------|
| 1 (base) | Absence of Trust | Invulnerability |
| 2 | Fear of Conflict | Artificial Harmony |
| 3 | Lack of Commitment | Ambiguity |
| 4 | Avoidance of Accountability | Low Standards |
| 5 (apex) | Inattention to Results | Status and Ego |

**Slide visuals per level:**
- Trust (slide 4): ice climber -- high-risk environment requiring trust
- Conflict (slide 5): two professionals in tense meeting
- Commitment (slide 6): **weasel** on a rock -- "slippery" people who avoid committing
- Accountability (slide 7): team ideation with lightbulb -- collaborative standards
- Results (slide 8): cartoon figures running in different directions -- individual agendas
- Review (slide 9): **chain** -- dysfunctions are linked; break one link and the whole chain fails

## 2. The Five Dysfunctions in Detail

### Level 1: Absence of Trust (Foundation)

**Root cause:** Unwillingness to be vulnerable -- an attempt to be **invulnerable**.

> *"Hiding our weaknesses, our mistakes and our humanity prevents the growth of trust and tries to hold everyone else at arm's length."* -- Graham/Lencioni

**Why it matters:** Trust is the foundation of teamwork and of all relationships. Teams operate in high-risk environments with pressure and difficulty. Without trust, effective collaboration on anything beyond the most trivial tasks is impossible.

> *"If the problem was simple, it could have been fixed by an individual and we wouldn't need a team."*

**Symptoms (from transcript + slides):**
- Slow to ask for help
- Slow to admit mistakes
- Hiding mistakes
- Acting defensively / ring-fencing work
- Slow to apologise
- Hold grudges, play politics
- Never sharing anything about personal life -- *"The message you're sending by remaining too private is that you don't trust team members with important information about you, either because you believe they will share it more widely or because you think that they will use the information to undermine you."*
- Not asking for help

**Antidotes (from slide 12):**
- Personal histories exercise (simple sharing exercises -- helps us to recognise the human in others)
- Team effectiveness exercise -- team identifies the most/least effective contributions others make to the team (this is a tough one!)
- Behavioural profiling tools (DISC, MBTI etc.)
- Anything that drives shared experiences (teambuilding etc.)

---

### Level 2: Fear of Conflict

**Root cause:** Trust is in short supply, so team members play it safe -- producing **artificial harmony**.

> *"You might think that the best teams are probably ones that agree with each other a lot, but this apparent harmony is really an artificial harmony."*

**Why it matters:** Trust enables passionate, unguarded dialogue around the challenges and opportunities the team faces. If everyone agrees, you have a weak team -- no diversity of views, which brings a deficit of creativity. Real teams rarely get universal agreement on difficult topics.

**Symptoms (from transcript + slides):**
- Boring meetings that only touch on less important or safe topics
- Difficult issues rarely discussed
- Unimportant topics discussed (instead of hard ones)
- Opinions of others not solicited
- Unpopular opinions never raised
- Important communication happens via **back channels and gossip** rather than open, transparent channels
- Conflictual discussions moved over quickly and left unresolved

**Antidotes (from slide 12):**
- Conflict mining -- someone who can arbitrate a dispute and help the team members to be able to share their points of view
- Real-time permission method

---

### Level 3: Lack of Commitment

**Root cause:** **Ambiguity** -- without debate, the team can't achieve clarity, so members can't commit.

> *"Commitment needs clarity and buy-in. Clarity because you need to know what you're signing up for and buy-in because you need to feel invested in what the team's doing."*

**Key insight -- consensus is NOT required:**

> *"Most team members don't expect the team should agree with their personal views about how things are done. What the members will object to, however, is when they don't feel like their voice has been heard or even asked for."*

**The weasel metaphor (slide 6 visual):** Those team members avoiding commitment are *"usually more slippery than a weasel and are typically the ones who hate action logs. They hate agreeing to due dates or writing down team objectives, as once things are written down, it's harder for them to renege on those agreements."*

**Symptoms (from transcript + slides):**
- Revisiting the same discussions again and again
- Missing new opportunities / late for everything
- Team members can't articulate the goals and plans of the team
- Each member has a different perception of what they think is supposed to happen
- Members unable to clearly articulate what the team is doing or where it is heading

**Antidotes (from slide 12):**
- Project pre-mortems
- Worst case analysis
- Cascading messaging

---

### Level 4: Avoidance of Accountability

**Root cause:** Without commitment to a clear plan, there is nothing to measure against -- **low standards** prevail.

> *"Accountability is about the willingness of team mates to call on their peers for behaviours or performance that hurt the team."*

> *"Hey, if I was never committed to the team's goals in the first place, how can you call me out for not meeting them?"*

**Key insight -- peer pressure beats bureaucracy:**

> *"Peer pressure in the team, providing there's a foundation of trust, is way more effective than a bureaucratic measurement system that attempts to get compliance through measurement."*

**The leader burden:** Without accountability within the team, the burden falls back on the team leader to be the sole source of all discipline and compliance. This is a particular problem with either a strong or weak hierarchy.

**Symptoms (from transcript + slides):**
- Mediocre performance
- Widely varying standards of performance
- Unequal performance and wide standards
- Resentment in both directions
- Requires regular interventions from team leader to ensure quality/output
- Needs bureaucratic systems to get compliance via measurement

**Antidotes (from slide 12):**
- Regular progress reviews
- Team rewards (not individual)
- Publication/recording goals and standards
- Team Effectiveness Exercise

---

### Level 5: Inattention to Results (Apex)

**Root cause:** Without accountability, members drift to their own **status and ego** -- individual needs and agendas overtake the group mission.

> *"Individual success and ego become more relevant than the success of the group and whilst this differs between different people, all humans have a tendency towards self-preservation that will grow unless it is kept in check by the accountability back to the group."*

**Academic departments as a prime example:** Academics are measured against their personal publishing record and profile rather than by what strength they bring to the organisation or how well they look after their students.

**Symptoms (from transcript + slides):**
- Team stagnates
- Fails to achieve its mission
- Rarely defeats competitors
- Focus on career and individual goals
- Ego/status driven behaviour

**Antidotes (from slide 12):**
- Results-based rewards
- Public declaration of results
- Team Scoreboard / Dashboard

### Interpretation

- **High scores** (>75% of max per category) = healthy functioning at that level
- **Low scores** (<50% of max) = dysfunction present, prioritise antidotes at this level
- Work bottom-up: fix Trust before addressing Conflict, fix Conflict before Commitment, etc.
- The pyramid is sequential -- a low score at a foundational level will typically drag down everything above it

## 9. Key Contextual Notes

**Why Lencioni's model uses dysfunctions, not virtues:** Most sociological models for business highlight positive attributes. Lencioni's popularity is *"partly attributable to its honesty in identifying the natural but unhelpful behaviours that humans can fall into when we have to work together."* By identifying dysfunction, we can recognise the opposite behaviours and choose to pursue those.

**The exercises may feel awkward:** Graham acknowledges *"you might find some of the exercises to be quite cheesy or awkward, but relationships between people are difficult and none more so than professional relationships. So the quicker you can move beyond the awkwardness of it all, the quicker you'll be able to focus on delivering results."*
