## SECTION 4 — AHEP4 M16 / M17 REQUIREMENTS

### 4.1 Verbatim standards (AHEP4 p36–37)

> **M16.** Function effectively as an individual, and as a member or leader of a team. **Evaluate effectiveness of own and team performance.**

> **M17.** Communicate effectively on complex engineering matters with technical and non-technical audiences, **evaluating the effectiveness of the methods used.**

### 4.2 The Master's-level increment (the single most important clause)

The Bachelor equivalents (C16/C17) drop the "evaluate effectiveness" clauses. **Those clauses are the Level 7 step-up.** A D7 that only *describes* what happened is Bachelor-level. A D7 that *evaluates effectiveness of methods/performance* — with a criterion, judgement, cause, and lesson — is Master-level.

**This is where 70+ marks live.** Missing the evaluation clause on EITHER M16 or M17 caps the report at ~65.

### 4.3 Sub-clause breakdown with evidence examples

#### M16.a — Function as an individual
- **Requires:** discrete, owned work to a professional standard; tangible outputs attributable to you (not "the team").
- **Evidence:** "I owned `vision.py` end-to-end from model selection through Pi deployment. Between 16 Feb and 31 Mar I delivered [N] commits and fixed the TFLite bbox coord bug on 30 Mar in 40 min after 3 h investigation."
- **Depth:** Tick-box. One quantified ownership paragraph suffices.

#### M16.b — Member of a team
- **Requires:** cross-interface collaboration, regular participation, integration work.
- **Evidence:** "I consumed the mechanical team's mount release v3.2 on 4 Mar and adapted `config.py` within 48 h, flagging a gimbal clearance issue fixed in v3.3."
- **Depth:** Tick-box.

#### M16.c — Leader of a team (OR — strictly optional at AHEP4 level)
- **Requires (IF claimed):** decision authority, delegation, influence under disagreement, meta-work.
- **Bristol L7 note:** "Leadership skills" is in the 70–79 Teamwork descriptor → for Bristol, leadership is **required** for 70+, not optional.
- **Evidence:** "On 16 Mar I decided the team should move from 640 to 1088 dataset despite the +6 h training cost, requiring [teammate] to redo the pipeline. Result: mAP50 0.87 → 0.995, justifying the call."

#### M16.d — Evaluate effectiveness (MASTER'S INCREMENT) — DEEP
- **Requires four elements:**
  1. **Criterion** — what "effective" means (velocity, bug rate, milestones hit, mAP, feedback)
  2. **Judgement** — actual score against criterion
  3. **Causal explanation** — why it scored that way
  4. **Lesson** — what changed as a result
- **Evidence (own):** "Target: 4.8 FPS by 15 Mar. Actual: 4.8 FPS on 11 Mar, but only after I abandoned the NCNN backend detour on 8 Mar (cost 2 days). Evaluation: on target, but 30% of my time went to a detour I should have scoped with a 2 h spike first. Lesson (applied 30 Mar): I now timebox all backend experiments to 4 h before committing."
- **Evidence (team):** "Against the D6 requirements matrix (12 Rs), the team delivered 12/12 in simulation but 4/12 in real hardware. 3 of 8 gaps were bench work we did not prioritise — a coordination failure, not an effort failure."
- **Depth: THE deepest clause. Aim for one paragraph on own evaluation and one on team evaluation, each with all four elements.**

#### M17.a — Communicate complex matters
- **Requires:** technically non-trivial content accurately conveyed.
- **Evidence:** "I wrote `docs/VISION_PIPELINE_FOR_COLLEAGUE.md` (2,400 words) explaining the TFLite → undistortion → GPS projection chain. Robbin deployed the model independently on 4 Apr using only this doc."

#### M17.b — Technical audience
- **Requires:** engineers/subject experts received and understood.
- **Evidence:** Supervisor comments, teammate able to pick up module from doc, code review with clarifying Qs closed out.

#### M17.c — Non-technical audience — DEEP (often the weak point)
- **Requires:** audience with NO engineering background, real stake, external to team.
- **Acceptable audiences:** SAR end-user, funder, public at open-day, journalist, comms officer, non-engineer regulator, non-engineering discipline teammate.
- **NOT acceptable:** another engineer in a different sub-discipline; the abstract of your own report; "my grandmother".
- **Adaptation evidence required — ≥2 of:**
  1. Jargon removal (side-by-side before/after: "mAP50" → "accuracy on test images")
  2. Analogy / framing ("the drone sees the dummy the way a human glance does")
  3. Visual substitution (photo replacing graph)
  4. Audience-first structure (leading with "why it matters")
  5. Feedback loop (showed draft → got reaction → revised)

#### M17.d — Evaluate methods (MASTER'S INCREMENT) — DEEP
- **Requires four elements:**
  1. **Enumerate methods** (report, diagram, demo, README, dashboard, video, code comments)
  2. **Criterion per method** (comprehension, time-to-understanding, feedback count, revision count)
  3. **Judgement** — what worked, what didn't
  4. **Comparison / alternative** — which method you'd use again and why
- **Evidence:** "I used three methods for the CV section: (a) narrative prose in D6; (b) annotated pipeline diagram (Fig 4.3); (c) live demo on 20 Mar. Against teammate comprehension (informal Q&A): prose produced 3 follow-up questions, diagram 0, demo 2. Diagram was most effective per minute of author effort. If I repeated, I would front-load the diagram and let prose support it."

### 4.4 Reflect vs Evaluate upgrade table

| Dimension | Reflect (Bachelor) | Evaluate (Master — AHEP4 M16/M17) |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Mode | Introspective, subjective | Judgemental against a criterion |
| Evidence | Feelings, impressions, anecdote | Measures, counts, ratings, outcomes |
| Output | "I felt that…" / "I noticed that…" | "Against [criterion], X scored Y because Z" |
| Direction | Looking back | Looking back **and** forward-actionable |
| Bristol L7 equivalent | "Some evidence of capacity to plan self-development" (50-59) | "Effective programme of self-development" (70-79) |

**Rule of thumb:** if you delete the sentence and what remains is only a feeling, it is reflection. Upgrade by adding: (a) a measure, (b) a comparison, (c) a consequence.

**Example upgrade:**
- Reflect: "I think my communication improved during the project."
- Evaluate: "My first D5 draft received 7 reviewer comments on clarity; my third draft received 1. The improvement came from adopting the claim-evidence-warrant paragraph structure I learned from the 16 Mar review. I applied the same structure to D6 §4.3 and this D7 §2."

### 4.5 Leadership threshold test (2 of 5 required if claimed)

You meet the threshold if you can evidence at least **TWO** of:

1. **Decision authority** — you made a call that bound the team (architecture, tool, deadline, scope cut)
2. **Delegation** — you assigned work to named others and it got done
3. **Influence under disagreement** — someone disagreed, you persuaded (not overruled)
4. **Accountability for group outcome** — your name attached to deliverables that were not solely yours
5. **Meta-work** — you improved how the team worked (process, tool, onboarding doc) not just what they produced

**Not leadership:** being the most active contributor; knowing the most; volunteering for tasks; being "the CV person".

**Safer language if threshold not met:** "I owned / was responsible for / drove / championed" — claims ownership without claiming leadership.

### 4.6 Non-technical audience threshold test (2 of 5 required)

Adaptation evidence — at least **TWO** of:
1. Jargon removal (before/after)
2. Analogy / framing
3. Visual substitution
4. Audience-first structure
5. Feedback loop with revision

Name the audience, name the artefact, show one concrete adaptation, report the reception.

## SECTION 5 — BRISTOL L7 HIDDEN REQUIREMENTS

These are NOT written in the brief but they are enforced by Bristol's Level 7 generic marking criteria. Missing any of them caps the mark as shown.

### 5.1 Conflict management — MANDATORY from 60+ Teamwork
- Bristol 60-69 Teamwork: "Works effectively within a team … **Able to manage conflict.**"

- Project without mentioned conflict raises a flag: either existed and hidden (dishonest) or insufficient team engagement (low insight).

- Bristol 70-79 Teamwork: "Consistently demonstrates effective teamworking **and leadership skills**…"

### 5.3 Media diversity — floor for 60+ Communication
- Bristol 60-69 Communication: "Can communicate effectively to a range of audiences, **using a wide range of media as appropriate.**"
- Only-the-written-report evidence → **CAP 59 on Communication.**
- Evidence ≥2 distinct media: report, diagram, code/README, demo, slide deck, video, dashboard, face-to-face meeting, sketch, photo annotation.
- 60-69 and 70-79 share the same descriptor text — the differentiation comes from AHEP4 M17.d evaluation.

### 5.4 Peer feedback — MANDATORY for 70+ Insight
- Bristol 70-79 Insight: "**Can provide effective feedback to others to aid their self-development.**"
- **Missing → CAP 68.**
- Feedback-giving template (Section 9, Move #3): name teammate + week + specific thing + your reasoning + their reaction + self-critique of your delivery + generalisable lesson.

### 5.5 Criterion + judgement + cause + lesson = "evaluate"
- This is the reflect-vs-evaluate differentiator (§4.4).
- Every major claim needs all four elements or it reads as "reflection" not "evaluation" and caps at ~65.

### 5.6 Bristol Decision-making 70+
- "Confident in adapting to changing and unfamiliar/challenging circumstances and making effective, evidence-based decisions."
- The **flight-day cancellation on 11 Mar** is a textbook unfamiliar circumstance. D7 must narrate at least one evidence-based decision in response (pivot to bench, scope revision, retraining on existing video).
- No challenge narrative → cap 59 on Decision-making axis.

### 5.7 Bristol Logical argument 60+
- "Ability to consider, critically evaluate and use alternative perspectives."
- Single-perspective narrative caps at 59.
- Every major claim should consider an alternative reading: "one reading is X; another is Y; I conclude X because…"

## SECTION 6 — THE 4-LAYER FRAMEWORK

**Do not pick one framework. Combine four, each doing what it is best at.**

### Layer 1 — Top-level tag: WWW/EBI
- Brief explicitly names it (p9). Using it signals "I read the brief".
- Maps 1:1 onto Insight rubric language ("assess own strengths and weaknesses").
- **Use as a tag on each sub-section**, not as the literal section title.
- Example: `My role — What went well. / Even better if.` (italicised, inline)

### Layer 2 — Per-anecdote micro-structure: STAR (+1 reflection sentence)
- Every concrete anecdote (~6-9 in the report) uses a compressed STAR:
  - **S** — Situation (1 sentence)
  - **T** — Task / responsibility (1 sentence)
  - **A** — Action, active voice, "I…" (1–2 sentences)
  - **R** — Result with a metric or observable outcome (1 sentence)
  - **Reflection** — starts with "Even better if" or "Next time" or "I now see" (1 sentence)
- **Budget:** ~100–120 words per anecdote.
- STAR carries Teamwork + Self-management evidence. Reflection sentence carries Insight.
- **Never drop the R.** A STAR without a measurable outcome is a CV bullet, not reflection.

### Layer 3 — Deep-dive (ONCE, in the self-development section): Kolb
- One story walked through all four Kolb stages with each stage **named in italics**:
  - *concrete experience* → *reflective observation* → *abstract conceptualisation* → *active experimentation*
- **Budget:** ~180 words.
- Recommended story: the solo-simulator → modular-interfaces → vision.py API + web GCS chain (textbook Kolb).
- Worth ~+2 marks for explicit theoretical literacy with application.
- **Do NOT use Gibbs** — 6 stages = 250-300 words and the Description/Feelings stages starve reflection.

### Layer 4 — Schön as a one-sentence label
- Drop the phrase "reflection-in-action (Schön)" once, as a parenthetical on the BGR camera debugging story ("I tested all 6 channel permutations rather than trust the API label").
- Costs 4 words, earns ~1 mark for theoretical literacy.
- **Do not expand.** Do not use as a section heading. Name-dropping without application is worthless.

### Frameworks to AVOID
- **Plus/Delta** — too terse, reads as bullet soup
- **Rolfe** — dominated by STAR for this project
- **CAR** — STAR's extra sentence is worth the 5–10 words for role clarity
- **Gibbs (as template)** — too long, 1 cycle eats 0.6 pages, over-dwells on Feelings

| Section | Words | Contents |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Header + intro | ~150 | Cover metadata, thesis/anchor opener (Section 8 Move 2) |
| My role (1a/b/c) | ~450 | 2 STAR anecdotes + proud statement + forward statement |

## SECTION 7 — THE 10 REQUIRED QUESTIONS (CHECKLIST)

Every question is a marked checkpoint. Missing or weak answers lose marks (–3 each, capped at –15).

### My role (3 questions — target ~450 words)

**1a — Roles and responsibilities (explicit vs implicit, did they change?)** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* "My explicit role at kickoff was [X]. Implicitly I was also given [Y] because [reason]. In wk N this shifted to [Z] when [incident]."
- *Evidence:* role title + one example of explicit work + one example of implicit work + one named shift

**1b — What are you most proud of?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* A specific artefact or moment (not "teamwork"), with a reason grounded in its impact.
- *Evidence:* named deliverable + measurable outcome + why it mattered to the team (not just you)

**1c — Focus differently in future?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* A specific behavioural change with a falsifiability test ("and the way I'll know it's working is…").
- *Evidence:* named weakness, named mechanism, named success signal

### My team (3 questions — target ~450 words)

**2a — Team structure, discussed explicitly? Changed?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* Named structure (e.g. "hub-and-spoke with a PM, 4 technical leads"), named discussion point (when structure was agreed), named change ("after [incident], we moved to…")
- *Evidence:* structure diagram OK in appendix, but body needs named roles

**2b — Most impactful team-working elements?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* Name ONE team-working practice (stand-up cadence, pair programming, decision log) and show its impact with evidence.
- *Evidence:* practice + metric / outcome / before-after observation

**2c — What would you change next time?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* A structural change with reasoning grounded in what went wrong, not a vague aspiration.
- *Evidence:* what broke + why it broke + specific fix

### My impact (2 questions — target ~450 words)

**3a — How did YOUR contributions drive the team forward?** [4 marks — weighted higher]
- *Strong answer:* 2–3 specific moments where your work unblocked or accelerated the team, with named dependencies.
- *Evidence:* named blocker + your action + downstream effect on named teammate's work

**3b — Add/refocus in future projects?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* Forward-looking with a mechanism (not "communicate better" — "keep a decision log").
- *Evidence:* named behaviour + signal of effectiveness

### My support (2 questions — target ~450 words)

**4a — What did others do that helped YOU?** [3 marks]
- *Strong answer:* Named teammate + specific help + what it unblocked in your work.
- *Evidence:* name + action + your downstream output

**4b — What did YOU do that helped OTHERS?** [4 marks — weighted higher] ← **the Insight 70+ gate lives here**
- *Strong answer:* Named teammate + specific feedback or artefact + their changed behaviour or output.
- *Evidence:* this is where the feedback-giving template (Section 9 Move #3) MUST land. Miss this → cap 68.

**Total question coverage = 32 marks** (the coverage gate, not the whole mark).

## SECTION 8 — DENSITY / PRESENTATION RULES

### Staging strategy
- **Working draft:** up to 7 pages — content first, cut later.
- **Final submission:** 5 pages hard — cut by merging paragraphs, moving detail to tables, tightening bullets, pulling the weakest anecdote per section.

### Visual targets
- Every page **85–95% filled**
- No section orphans (heading at page bottom with content starting next page)
- No widow lines (single-line tail of a paragraph)
- Balanced visual weight left-to-right and top-to-bottom
- Paragraphs 3–5 sentences (shorter = more density-per-reflection-move)

### Readability floor (HARD — never compress past these)

| Element | Floor |
|---------|-------|
| Body font size | 10pt or 11pt (never below 10pt) |
| Line spacing | `\linespread{1.05}` (never below) |
| Table font | `\small` (9pt) minimum, `\footnotesize` only in edge cases |
| Paragraph spacing | `\parskip = 0.3em` (visible breathing room) |
| Margins | 1.8–2 cm (never below 1.5 cm — looks amateurish) |
| Column gap (multicols) | `\columnsep = 1em` |

### Density tactics
- Compact tables with `\arraystretch{1.05}`, `tabularx`, `p{}` columns
- Two-column `multicols` for lists that don't need full width
- Mini-headings inline: `\textbf{My role.}` at paragraph start saves a line
- Inline evidence markers: `[commit 3d62e6a]`, `[wk20]`, `[2026-03-11 field]`
- `enumitem` with `itemsep=0pt, topsep=2pt, partopsep=0pt` for compact lists
- Margin annotations (`marginpar` / `sidenotes`) for rubric tags (1a, 2b) without eating line budget

### Screenshot loop (iterate visually)
After each rewrite:
1. Compile → PDF
2. `pdftoppm -r 150 main.pdf page` → PNGs
3. Visually check: whitespace? orphans? overcrowding?
4. Tweak, recompile, re-screenshot

### Headings
- Exactly 4 top-level: **My role / My team / My impact / My support** (or near-verbatim variant)
- Functional, not creative ("Reflection on Teamwork and Leadership" is creative; "My role" is functional)
- Bold, visually distinct, consistent hierarchy throughout
- No decorative section dividers

### References and appendices
- **References optional.** 2–6 is normal for a D7 at 70+. Used to anchor reflection (Schön, Kolb, Hatton & Smith, Moon, Tuckman) — not decoration.
- Every reference in the bibliography **must be cited in the body**. Unused refs → CAP 65.
- **Appendices optional.** Acceptable: self-evaluation matrix, dev log, incident cross-ref table. **Not acceptable:** D6 tech diagrams, team photos, CV-style content.
- Every appendix **must be referenced from the body**. Orphan appendices = padding penalty.

## SECTION 9 — HATTON & SMITH LEVEL 4 MARKERS

Hatton & Smith's 4-level model is the canonical depth rubric.

| Level | Name | Example | Mark band |
|-------|------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Descriptive writing | "We built X. I did Y. The team met on Tuesdays." | Fail / low Pass |
| 2 | Descriptive reflection | "I did Y because I thought it would help." (reasons given but unexamined) | Pass / low Merit |
| 3 | Dialogic reflection | "I did Y because I thought it would help — but in hindsight the team needed Z, and I now see I chose Y partly because it was familiar." (multiple perspectives) | Merit / low Distinction |
| 4 | **Critical reflection** | Level 3 + explicit examination of assumptions, frames of reference, connection to theory or wider context | **Distinction (70+)** |

### The depth test

**Level 3 hits ~68. Level 4 hits 72+.** The rewriter must push every reflective paragraph from Level 3 to Level 4 by adding the **"and I now see this is because…"** move.

Every page of the rewritten D7 should contain at least **two Level-4 moves** from the list below.

### Ten concrete phrase templates (use literally)

1. **"The uncomfortable realisation was that…"** — forces Level 4 by naming a belief-revision moment.

2. **"I initially framed this as [X] — in fact it was [Y], and I now see I preferred the [X] framing because it protected me from [Z]."** — reframing + motive.

3. **"I told [name] in week [N] that [specific thing]. Their reaction was [honest]. My delivery was [self-critical analysis]. What I would do differently is [concrete mechanism]."** — THE Insight-72+ feedback template.

4. **"Had I acted on [X] a week earlier, we would have saved [specific measurable outcome]. The reason I did not was [honest reasoning], a pattern I now recognise in myself."** — counterfactual + pattern.

5. **"This experience forced me to reframe my belief that [previous]. I now think [new] — and the evidence that convinced me was [specific incident]."** — explicit belief revision.

6. **"I was wrong about [specific claim]. [Teammate] was right. I dismissed their view in [specific moment] because [honest reason]. The lesson is not that I should always defer — it is that I should [specific mechanism]."** — admitting error without collapsing into humility.

7. **"What looked like a technical problem was actually a [communication / trust / incentive] problem, because [mechanism]."** — surface-to-root-cause.

8. **"The tension between my commitment to [value A] and my commitment to [value B] surfaced in [specific incident]. I had not previously noticed these two values could conflict in me."** — self-discovery (rare, highly rewarded).

9. **"I will [specific behavioural change] in future team projects, and the way I will know whether it is working is [observable signal]."** — falsifiable forward commitment.

10. **"I leave this project believing that engineering leadership is less about [what I used to think] and more about [what I now think]. The moment this became clear to me was [specific incident]."** — identity-shift closing.

### Opener patterns (Distinction-level)

Do ONE of these — never start with "This report will reflect on…":

1. **Drop into a specific scene.** "At 23:47 on the night before the flight demo, I was in the lab deleting my own code."
2. **State the thesis.** "This project taught me that my reflex to solve problems alone is not a strength — it is a failure mode that only looked like a strength in smaller-scale work."
3. **Name the uncomfortable truth.** "I will argue in this report that the thing I am most proud of — the autonomous state machine — is also the thing that most exposed a weakness I had not previously been willing to see."

### Closer patterns (Distinction-level)

Do ALL of these:
1. Name what will actually change, with a **mechanism** not a wish.
2. Acknowledge what is still unresolved (intellectual honesty).
3. Connect the personal lesson to professional identity (Move 10).
4. **Avoid triumph.** Distinction closings end with "here is the lesson I will carry", not "and it was a success".

## SECTION 10 — 20 FAILURE MODES TO AVOID

The rewriter must scan for and eliminate every one of these. Each is a direct cause of dropping from 70s to 60s.

1. **Chronological trap.** Structuring week-by-week or phase-by-phase. Forces description, starves reflection. → Structure by theme or lesson.
2. **Generalised teamwork platitudes.** "Communication is key." "Teamwork is important." → Automatic Merit ceiling.
3. **Hidden self-praise.** "My main weakness was caring too much about quality." → Humblebrag, kills credibility.
4. **Unsupported leadership claims.** "I took a leadership role." Where? When? What changed? → Downgrade to "owned".
5. **Conflict-free narrative.** No disagreements, no friction. → Assume dishonesty or low insight.
6. **Feedback in passive voice.** "Feedback was provided." Who? To whom? Reaction? → First-person specific.
7. **Defensive retract.** "I could have done X but the constraints made it unavoidable." → Erases the reflection.
8. **Triumphalism.** "The project was a huge success and I am proud." → Distinction closings are sober.
9. **Tools-and-tech padding.** "I learned Python, OpenCV, MAVLink." → CV bullet, not reflection.
10. **Citing Gibbs/Kolb without using.** Decorative citations with no structural consequence. → Worthless.
11. **Vague future commitments.** "I will communicate better." → No mechanism = no credit.
12. **Hiding behind the team.** "We decided…" "We realised…" → Must be "I" in every reflective paragraph.
13. **Metrics as substitute for reflection.** Progress charts, contribution tables. → Not reflection.
14. **Perfect-past syndrome.** Describing past events as if always obvious in the moment. → Distinction writers are honest about what they didn't see until later.
15. **Feedback-free feedback paragraphs.** Claiming to have given feedback without naming what or how it landed.
16. **Equating "learning about X" with "reflecting on X".** Learning is input. Reflection is what changed.
17. **Gibbs as rigid 6-heading template.** Scaffold OK, reproducing all 6 headings screams undergraduate.
18. **Bulleted lists where prose is needed.** Reflection lives in sentences. Lists kill nuance.
19. **Closing with a lessons-learned bullet list.** Project-management artefact, not reflection. Prose only in the conclusion.
20. **Starting with "This report will…"** The most common Merit-ceiling opener. **Banned.**

## SECTION 11 — EVIDENCE BANK (12 LOAD-BEARING ITEMS FOR 70+)

A 70+ D7 must contain at least the following **twelve named evidence items**. Items **5, 6, 8, 9** are load-bearing — missing any of them caps at 68.

1. **One quantified individual-delivery metric** — files owned, commits, tests, milestones hit, mAP achieved.
2. **One specific cross-team collaboration** with named interface (CAD release v3.2, mount clearance, etc.).
3. **One decision made under disagreement or uncertainty** — flight cancellation pivot, model swap, threshold tuning, scope cut.
4. **One conflict / friction episode and its resolution** — named parties, named mechanism (not "better communication").
5. **One self-evaluation paragraph** with criterion + judgement + cause + lesson. **[LOAD-BEARING]**
6. **One team-evaluation paragraph** with criterion + judgement + cause + lesson. **[LOAD-BEARING]**
7. **Two distinct communication media used**, each with an audience named.
8. **One non-technical audience named** with a concrete adaptation (2/5 threshold). **[LOAD-BEARING]**
9. **One A/B or before/after comparison** of communication methods. **[LOAD-BEARING]**
10. **One peer-development action** — feedback given, doc written for onboarding, code review with applied outcome.
11. **One applied lesson with measurable outcome inside the project timeline** (not just "next time").
12. **One forward-looking CPD/career plan** tied to an identified weakness, with falsifiability test.

If the draft lacks any of **5, 6, 8, 9** it will not reach 70.

## SECTION 13 — PER-PARAGRAPH SCORING HEURISTIC

For every paragraph the rewriter produces, run the 7-point test:

1. **Is there a specific named incident?** If no → rewrite.
2. **Is "I" the subject, not "we"?** If no → rewrite.
3. **Does it revise a belief, name an error, or reframe a prior view?** If no → Merit ceiling.
4. **Is the self-criticism specific, actionable, proportionate?** If no → rewrite. (Section 4 Moon/H&S criteria.)
5. **Does it avoid all 20 failure modes (§10)?** If no → rewrite.
6. **Is the forward-looking commitment falsifiable?** If no → add a mechanism / observable signal.
7. **Would a cynical marker be able to say "but did this actually change anything?"** If yes → add evidence of applied change.

**Target:** every page contains ≥2 Level-4 moves from §9 and zero failure modes from §10.

### Type of self-criticism that is rewarded
- **Specific:** name the behaviour not the trait. ("I dominated the wk3 meeting by interrupting Alex twice" — NOT "I can be impatient".)
- **Actionable:** paired with a concrete mechanism tested against actual project evidence.
- **Proportionate:** don't flagellate over trivia; don't minimise real mistakes.
- **Forward-looking:** lesson already changed behaviour within the project.
- **Structural, not dispositional:** identify a pattern/system problem, not a personality flaw.
