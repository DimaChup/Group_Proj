## 2. WEAPONIZABLE PHRASES (pattern-match triggers for markers)

### 83-100 Band Triggers
1. **"outstanding ability to work and LEAD"** — "lead" is the verb. Not "contributed to" or "supported". Active direction of others.
2. **"CREATIVITY and FLEXIBILITY responsive to group members' interests"** — You adapted to THEM (not they to you). Shows servant-leadership.
3. **"CONFIDENCE in working autonomously and SETTING OWN GOALS"** — Self-set goals = you defined targets beyond the brief.
4. **"effective feedback to others to AID THEIR SELF-DEVELOPMENT"** — Their development, not team output. Personal growth of a teammate you helped.

### 72-78 Band Triggers
5. **"CONSISTENTLY demonstrates"** — Pattern word. One anecdote = Merit. Two+ with a thread = Distinction.
6. **"leadership skills"** — Not "leadership role". Skills = capability demonstrated, not title held.
7. **"ENSURE teams work effectively"** — You guaranteed outcomes, not just participated. Process/structure evidence.
8. **"works AUTONOMOUSLY"** — You decided what to do, not just how to do assigned work.
9. **"IMPLEMENT an effective PROGRAMME"** — Two gated words: implement (past tense, done) + programme (systematic, not ad hoc).
10. **"Can PROVIDE effective feedback to others"** — Active giving, not passive receipt. Named instance required.

### 62-68 Band Triggers
11. **"recognising the VALUE and CONTRIBUTIONS of others"** — Must explicitly name what others did well.
12. **"Able to MANAGE conflict"** — Manage =/= avoid. Named mechanism required.
13. **"PROFESSIONAL attitude"** — Quality beyond minimum. Standards language.
14. **"CONFIDENT in self-reflection"** — No hedging ("I think maybe I could have..."). Direct, owned analysis.
15. **"PROACTIVE approach to self-development"** — You initiated, not responded to feedback.

### Usage rule
Echo these exact words in your draft. Markers scan with rubric adjacent. If they read "I consistently demonstrated leadership by ensuring the team met our milestones" they tick 72-78. If they read "I helped the team" they tick 52-58.

## 3. SILENT LEVEL 7 THRESHOLDS (Bristol convention, NOT in brief)

These are enforced but never explicitly stated in the D7 brief. Sourced from Bristol's generic Level 7 marking criteria and AHEP4 M16/M17.

### 3.1 Conflict management — GATE from 62+ Teamwork
- **Rubric evidence (p12):** "Able to manage conflict" appears in 62-68 AND 72-78 AND 83-100. It does NOT appear in 52-58.
- **Implication:** No conflict = cap 58 on Teamwork. No exceptions.
- **Hidden trap:** A report that claims "our team had no conflicts" signals either dishonesty or insufficient engagement. Markers view BOTH negatively.

### 3.2 Leadership — GATE from 72+ Teamwork
- **Rubric evidence (p12):** "leadership skills" appears only in 72-78 and 83-100.
- **AHEP4 says "member OR leader"** — optional. Bristol rubric does NOT say "or" — it says "and leadership skills". Bristol overrides AHEP4 here.
- **Implication:** No leadership evidence = cap 68 on Teamwork regardless of everything else.

### 3.3 Peer feedback — GATE from 72+ Insight
- **Rubric evidence (p12):** "Can provide effective feedback to others to aid their self-development" appears in 72-78 and 83-100.
- **Implication:** No named feedback-giving episode = cap 68 on Insight.
- **Not enough:** "I gave feedback when needed" — must name the teammate, the specific feedback, and what changed.

### 3.4 "Implement" not "plan" — GATE from 72+ Insight
- **Rubric evidence (p12):** 72-78 says "implement an effective programme of self-development."
- **"Implement" = past tense, done.** Future promises ("I plan to...") stay in 62-68 ("proactive approach").
- **Implication:** Self-development that is only planned/intended = cap 68. Must show completed actions with measured outcomes.

### 3.5 "Evaluate" not "reflect" — Level 7 increment
- **Source:** AHEP4 M16 says "Evaluate effectiveness of own and team performance." The Bachelor equivalent (C16) only says "contribute to."
- **Implication:** Description + feelings = Bachelor-level = cap ~60. Criterion + judgement + cause + lesson = Level 7 = 70+.
- **Test:** Can you delete the sentence and lose only a feeling? It's reflection, not evaluation. Rewrite.

### 3.6 Autonomy — GATE from 72+ Self-management
- **Rubric evidence (p12):** "Works autonomously" first appears in 72-78. Not in 62-68.
- **Implication:** All work assigned/directed by others = cap 68 on Self-management. Must show self-initiated work.

### 3.7 Decision-making under unfamiliar circumstances
- **Source:** Bristol generic criteria, Decision-making 70+: "Confident in adapting to changing and unfamiliar/challenging circumstances and making effective, evidence-based decisions."
- **Implication:** No challenge/pivot/uncertainty narrative = capped below 70 on decision-making axis.

## 4. BRIEF PHRASES THAT ARE SECRETLY GATES (p09)

### 4.1 "you are free to discuss specifics"
- **Surface reading:** Permission to name names.
- **Secret gate:** This is an INSTRUCTION disguised as permission. No specifics = generic report = cap 58. The brief says "free to discuss" because it expects you WILL. Markers read unspecific reports as evasive.
- **Minimum specifics for 70+:** 5+ named teammates, 3+ dated incidents, 1+ named conflict.

### 4.2 "what you did to INFLUENCE the team's working practices and outputs"
- **Key word: INFLUENCE.** Not "contribute to." Not "participate in."
- **Influence verbs required:** decided, persuaded, proposed (and it was adopted), restructured, redirected, challenged, demonstrated (and others followed).
- **Non-influence verbs that cap at 62:** helped, contributed, worked on, supported, assisted.
- **Secret gate:** A report where "I" only does assigned work = cap 62 (no influence shown). Must show team trajectory changed because of YOUR action.

### 4.3 "how you DEVELOPED"
- **Key word: DEVELOPED.** Past tense. Tangible growth.
- **Required evidence:** Before-state → trigger → after-state with measurable difference.
- **Not enough:** "I learned a lot about teamwork." That's a claim, not development evidence.
- **Secret gate:** No before/after growth narrative = cap 62 on Insight.

### 4.4 "what you MIGHT DO DIFFERENTLY in comparable future situations"
- **Key word: MIGHT.** This sounds optional. It is NOT.
- **Every section needs a "differently" answer.** 1c, 2c, 3b all explicitly ask for it. 4b implicitly does.
- **Secret gate:** Missing forward-looking statements = -3 per missing question.
- **Distinction upgrade:** Add a falsifiability test ("and the way I'll know it's working is...").

### 4.5 "reflecting on your specific experiences, within your specific team"
- **Double "specific."** The brief emphasizes this word twice in one sentence.
- **Secret gate:** Generic/transferable reflections ("in any team, communication is important") = cap 58. Every reflection must be rooted in a named incident from THIS project with THESE people.

### 4.6 "constructive reflection ... 'what went well' and 'even better if'"
- **The brief explicitly names WWW/EBI.** Using it signals "I read the brief." Not using it is not penalized, but using it earns implicit alignment credit.
- **Secret gate:** All-positive reports (no "even better if") = cap 62 on Insight (no self-criticism).

### 4.7 "You may wish to refer to concepts detailed in the Professional Practice slides"
- **"May wish to" = should.** Academic code for "do this."
- **The named frameworks from PP lectures are Tuckman, Belbin, Kolb, Schon, WWW/EBI.** Using 1-2 with application = 72+. Using 0 = no theoretical literacy = cap 65.
- **Trap:** Naming a framework without applying it = worse than not naming it. Decorative citation = cap 65.

### 4.8 "the focus of this reflection is on what YOU did"
- **"You" is italicized in the brief (bold).** Three "you"s in one sentence.
- **Secret gate:** "We" as dominant pronoun = cap 52 on all criteria. Every reflective paragraph must have "I" as subject.

### 4.9 Assessment against "relevant Level 7 criteria" (p09 footnote)
- **The footnote links to Bristol's full marking criteria.** This is not decoration — it means the GENERIC Level 7 descriptors (Decision-making, Communication, Logical argument) all apply as hidden assessment axes even though only Teamwork/Self-management/Insight are in the D7-specific rubric.
- **Secret gate:** The full Bristol L7 grid has ~8 axes. The D7 rubric shows 3. The other 5 can still cap marks via "tailored rubric" interpretation.

## 6. TONE TRAPS

### 6.1 AI Boilerplate (immediate detection, cap 48)
- Neutral hedging: "It could be argued that..." / "One might consider..."
- Perfect paragraph structure with topic sentence + 3 supports + conclusion
- Absence of specific names, dates, emotions
- **Fix:** Write ugly first. Add a typo you fix. Use contractions. Name feelings ("I was frustrated").

### 6.2 Humble Brag (cap 62 on Insight)
- "My main weakness is that I care too much about quality"
- "I sometimes take on too much because I want everything to be perfect"
- "I could have delegated more but I knew I could do it faster"
- **Fix:** Name a REAL weakness that cost the team something. "I dominated the architecture decisions in weeks 2-4, which meant Edward's mechanical integration proposals were not heard until week 5, costing us a redesign."

### 6.3 Triumphalism (cap 65 on Insight)
- "The project was a huge success"
- "I am proud of what we achieved"
- "Our team overcame every challenge"
- **Fix:** Distinction closings are sober. "I leave this project with a clearer understanding of [X], and an unresolved question about [Y]."

### 6.4 "We" dominance (cap 52 on all criteria)
- More than 30% "we" sentences in reflective paragraphs
- **Fix:** Every reflective sentence starts with "I". Use "we" only for factual context ("We had 5 members").

### 6.5 Vague Future (cap 62 on Insight)
- "I will communicate better in future"
- "I plan to develop my leadership skills"
- "Next time I would be more organised"
- **Fix:** Add mechanism + falsifiability: "I will keep a decision log updated within 24h of each team meeting, and review it fortnightly. The signal that this is working is that no decision is revisited due to ambiguity."

### 6.6 Technical Showcase (cap 58 on Insight)
- Describing what the code does, not what you learned
- "I implemented a YOLOv8 pipeline with TFLite backend achieving 4.8 FPS"
- **Fix:** This is D6 content. D7 is about what the experience TAUGHT you about yourself and teamwork. The tech is context, never the point.

### 6.7 Conflict Avoidance (cap 58 on Teamwork)
- "Our team worked well together with no major disagreements"
- "We resolved issues through open communication"
- **Fix:** Name the disagreement, name the parties, name your role, name the mechanism, name the outcome.

### 6.8 Passive Voice Feedback (cap 68 on Insight)
- "Constructive feedback was exchanged"
- "We had regular feedback sessions"
- **Fix:** "I told Robin in week 20 that his serial protocol was blocking integration. I suggested he switch to UDP. He did, and Demetro's dashboard connected the same evening."

## 7. TOP 10 THINGS MISSING FROM CURRENT SCORING DOC FOR 85+

The D7_SCORING_FINAL.md is already excellent (one of the most thorough rubric analyses I've seen). These are the remaining gaps:

### 7.1 No worked example of an 83+ paragraph
The doc has templates but no complete model paragraph that scores 83+. A single 120-word exemplar paragraph hitting all 7 paragraph-test criteria would be worth more than 3 pages of theory.

### 7.2 "Responsive to group members' interests" is under-analyzed
The 83+ Teamwork descriptor says "responsive to group members' interests" — this means you adapted YOUR plan/approach because a teammate had a different preference, interest, or need. This is servant-leadership, not just flexibility. No evidence pattern is given for this.

### 7.3 "Setting own goals" is under-analyzed
The 83+ Insight descriptor says "confidence in ... setting own goals." This means self-imposed targets BEYOND the brief/assignment. The current doc doesn't distinguish between meeting assigned goals (72) and setting your own (83+).

### 7.4 No guidance on appendix strategy
The brief says "excluding cover page and appendices." Appendices are free pages. A self-evaluation matrix, development timeline, or incident cross-reference table in an appendix can carry evidence that doesn't fit in 5 pages. The current doc mentions this in passing but doesn't give an appendix budget or structure.

### 7.5 No explicit "how to show consistency"
72+ requires "consistently demonstrates." The current doc says "two+ with a thread" but doesn't show how to structurally create the impression of consistency in 5 pages (e.g., a running thread across all 4 sections, or a summary table of 6+ incidents).

### 7.6 Communication evaluation (M17.d) evidence pattern is weak
The current doc gives one example but M17.d is the hardest clause to hit. Need: a before/after of the SAME content presented two ways, with reception metrics (questions asked, follow-up needed, time-to-understanding).

### 7.7 No "adversity as opportunity" framing guide
The 11 Mar weather cancellation, the TFLite bbox bug, the corrupt calibration file — these are GIFTS for reflection. The doc mentions them as evidence items but doesn't explain that adversity narratives are the #1 vehicle for Level-4 moves because they naturally contain belief-revision.

### 7.8 No guidance on emotional register
83+ requires genuine vulnerability without melodrama. The doc bans triumphalism and humble brags but doesn't guide toward the correct register: measured honesty with structural self-criticism.

### 7.9 Peer feedback quality ladder missing
There's a spectrum: (1) "I gave feedback" (cap 58), (2) "I told X to do Y" (cap 62), (3) "I told X that Y, here's why, their reaction was Z" (72), (4) "I told X that Y, here's why, they changed Z, I evaluated my delivery and would adjust W" (83+). This ladder isn't explicit.

### 7.10 No analysis of what "outstanding" means structurally
83+ uses "outstanding" in all three criteria. At Bristol Level 7, "outstanding" = top 5-10% of cohort. Structurally this means: every element that 72 requires, done better, plus the 83+ unique phrases (creativity, flexibility, responsive, setting own goals, confidence). The doc lists phrases but doesn't show how to weave them into a 5-page structure.
