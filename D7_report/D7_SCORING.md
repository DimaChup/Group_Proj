## 3. THE 10 SUB-QUESTIONS

Every question is a marked checkpoint. Missing or weak answers lose marks (-3 each, capped at -15 total).

### My role (3 questions -- target ~450 words)

**1a. Roles and responsibilities** (explicit vs implicit, did they change?)
- Verbatim: *"What were your roles and responsibilities in this project? Which of these were explicitly allocated, and which did you take on/were you given implicitly? Did they change over the course of the project?"*
- Evidence required: role title + one explicit allocation + one implicit pickup + one named shift with date

**1b. Most proud of**
- Verbatim: *"What elements of your contribution are you most happy about and/or proud of?"*
- Evidence required: specific artefact or moment + measurable impact + why it mattered to the team (not just to you)

**1c. Focus differently in future**
- Verbatim: *"What different things might you focus on in your future work?"*
- Evidence required: specific behavioural change + mechanism + falsifiability test ("the way I'll know it's working is...")

### My team (3 questions -- target ~450 words)

**2a. Team structure** (discussed explicitly? changed?)
- Verbatim: *"How was your team structured? Was this discussed explicitly? What changed over the course of the project?"*
- Evidence required: named structure + when it was agreed + named change after an incident

**2b. Most impactful team-working elements**
- Verbatim: *"What elements of team working do you think were most impactful on your outputs?"*
- Evidence required: ONE named practice + metric or before/after observation showing impact

**2c. Change next time**
- Verbatim: *"What would you change if you were to do a similar project in future?"*
- Evidence required: what broke + why it broke + specific structural fix (not vague aspiration)

### My impact (2 questions -- target ~450 words)

**3a. Contributions driving team forward** [weighted higher]
- Verbatim: *"How did your contributions drive the team forward and contribute to success?"*
- Evidence required: 2-3 specific moments where your work unblocked or accelerated the team + named dependencies

**3b. Add/refocus in future**
- Verbatim: *"What might you add or re-focus on in future projects?"*
- Evidence required: named behaviour + mechanism + signal of effectiveness

### My support (2 questions -- target ~450 words)

**4a. What others did for you**
- Verbatim: *"What did other team members do that helped you work more effectively?"*
- Evidence required: named teammate + specific help + what it unblocked in your work

**4b. What you did for others** [weighted higher -- the Insight 70+ gate lives here]
- Verbatim: *"What actions did you take that helped other individuals and/or the team as a whole work more effectively?"*
- Evidence required: named teammate + specific feedback or artefact + their observed behaviour change. This is where the feedback-giving template MUST land. Miss this = cap 68.

## 5. HIDDEN LEVEL 7 THRESHOLDS

These are NOT written in the brief but they are enforced by Bristol's Level 7 generic marking criteria. Missing any caps the mark as shown.

### 5.1 Conflict management -- MANDATORY from 60+ Teamwork

Bristol 62-68 Teamwork: "Able to manage conflict."
- **Missing = CAP 58.**
- A project without mentioned conflict raises a flag: either existed and was hidden (dishonest) or insufficient engagement (low insight).
- Evidence pattern: name the disagreement, name the parties, name your action, name the outcome. Never claim "resolved by better communication" -- name the actual mechanism.

### 5.2 Leadership -- MANDATORY for 70+ Teamwork (NOT OPTIONAL)

Bristol 72-78 Teamwork: "Consistently demonstrates effective teamworking **and leadership skills**..."
- AHEP4 says "member **or** leader" (optional). Bristol does NOT -- leadership is required for 70+.
- **Missing = CAP 68 on Teamwork.**
- Must pass the 2/5 leadership threshold test (see 6.5).

### 5.3 Media diversity -- floor for 60+ Communication

Bristol 60-69 Communication: "using a wide range of media as appropriate."
- Only-the-written-report evidence = **CAP 59 on Communication.**
- Evidence 2+ distinct media: report, diagram, code/README, demo, slide deck, video, dashboard, meeting, photo annotation.

### 5.4 Peer feedback -- MANDATORY for 70+ Insight

Bristol 72-78 Insight: "**Can provide effective feedback to others to aid their self-development.**"
- **Missing = CAP 68 on Insight.**
- Template: named teammate + week + specific thing + your reasoning + their reaction + self-critique of delivery + generalisable lesson.
- "I gave constructive feedback when needed" is the #1 Merit-ceiling sentence. Never write it.

### 5.5 Evaluate =/= reflect -- the Master's-level differentiator

Level 7 requires: **criterion + judgement + cause + lesson**. Feelings alone are Bachelor-level.
- Missing = CAP ~65.
- Every major claim needs all four elements or it reads as "reflection" not "evaluation".

### 5.6 Bristol Decision-making 70+

"Confident in adapting to changing and unfamiliar/challenging circumstances and making effective, evidence-based decisions."
- The flight-day cancellation on 11 Mar is a textbook unfamiliar circumstance. Must narrate at least one evidence-based decision in response.
- No challenge narrative = CAP 59 on Decision-making axis.

### 5.7 Bristol Logical argument 60+

"Ability to consider, critically evaluate and use alternative perspectives."
- Single-perspective narrative = CAP 59.
- Every major claim should consider: "one reading is X; another is Y; I conclude X because..."

## 6. AHEP4 M16 / M17

### 6.1 Verbatim standards (AHEP4 p36-37)

> **M16.** Function effectively as an individual, and as a member or leader of a team. **Evaluate effectiveness of own and team performance.**

> **M17.** Communicate effectively on complex engineering matters with technical and non-technical audiences, **evaluating the effectiveness of the methods used.**

### 6.2 The Master's-level increment

Bachelor equivalents (C16/C17) drop the "evaluate effectiveness" clauses. **Those clauses are the Level 7 step-up.** A D7 that only *describes* is Bachelor-level. A D7 that *evaluates effectiveness* -- with criterion, judgement, cause, lesson -- is Master-level.

**This is where 70+ marks live.** Missing the evaluation clause on EITHER M16 or M17 caps at ~65.

### 6.3 Sub-clause breakdown

#### M16.a -- Function as an individual
- Requires: discrete, owned work to a professional standard; tangible outputs attributable to you.
- Depth: tick-box. One quantified ownership paragraph suffices.

#### M16.b -- Member of a team
- Requires: cross-interface collaboration, regular participation, integration work.
- Depth: tick-box.

#### M16.c -- Leader of a team (OR -- optional at AHEP4, REQUIRED at Bristol 70+)
- Requires (if claimed): decision authority, delegation, influence under disagreement, meta-work.
- Safer language if threshold not met: "I owned / was responsible for / drove / championed" -- claims ownership without claiming leadership.

#### M16.d -- Evaluate effectiveness (MASTER'S INCREMENT) -- DEEP
Requires four elements:
1. **Criterion** -- what "effective" means (velocity, bug rate, milestones, mAP, feedback)
2. **Judgement** -- actual score against criterion
3. **Causal explanation** -- why it scored that way
4. **Lesson** -- what changed as a result

Need one paragraph on own evaluation AND one on team evaluation, each with all four elements.

#### M17.a -- Communicate complex matters
- Requires: technically non-trivial content accurately conveyed.
- Depth: tick-box.

#### M17.b -- Technical audience
- Requires: engineers/subject experts received and understood.
- Depth: tick-box.

#### M17.c -- Non-technical audience -- DEEP
- Requires: audience with NO engineering background, real stake, external to team.
- NOT acceptable: another engineer in a different sub-discipline; the abstract of your own report.
- Adaptation evidence required -- at least 2 of 5:
  1. Jargon removal (before/after)
  2. Analogy / framing
  3. Visual substitution (photo replacing graph)
  4. Audience-first structure
  5. Feedback loop (showed draft, got reaction, revised)

#### M17.d -- Evaluate methods (MASTER'S INCREMENT) -- DEEP
Requires four elements:
1. **Enumerate methods** (report, diagram, demo, README, dashboard, video, code comments)
2. **Criterion per method** (comprehension, time-to-understanding, feedback count)
3. **Judgement** -- what worked, what didn't
4. **Comparison / alternative** -- which method you'd use again and why

### 6.4 Reflect vs Evaluate upgrade table

| Dimension | Reflect (Bachelor) | Evaluate (Master) |
|---|---|---|
| Mode | Introspective, subjective | Judgemental against a criterion |
| Evidence | Feelings, impressions, anecdote | Measures, counts, ratings, outcomes |
| Output | "I felt that..." / "I noticed that..." | "Against [criterion], X scored Y because Z" |
| Direction | Looking back | Looking back **and** forward-actionable |
| Bristol L7 | "Some evidence of capacity to plan" (50-59) | "Effective programme of self-development" (70-79) |

**Example upgrade:**
- Reflect: "I think my communication improved during the project."
- Evaluate: "My first D5 draft received 7 reviewer comments on clarity; my third draft received 1. The improvement came from adopting claim-evidence-warrant paragraph structure from the 16 Mar review. I applied the same structure to D6 4.3 and this D7."

### 6.5 Leadership threshold test (2 of 5 required if claimed)

1. **Decision authority** -- you made a call that bound the team
2. **Delegation** -- you assigned work to named others and it got done
3. **Influence under disagreement** -- someone disagreed, you persuaded (not overruled)
4. **Accountability for group outcome** -- your name attached to deliverables not solely yours
5. **Meta-work** -- you improved how the team worked, not just what they produced

**Not leadership:** being the most active contributor; knowing the most; volunteering for tasks.

### 6.6 Non-technical audience threshold test (2 of 5 required)

1. Jargon removal (before/after)
2. Analogy / framing
3. Visual substitution
4. Audience-first structure
5. Feedback loop with revision

Name the audience, name the artefact, show one concrete adaptation, report the reception.

## 7. HATTON & SMITH LEVEL 4

### 7.1 The 4-level depth model

| Level | Name | Example | Band |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Descriptive writing | "We built X. I did Y." | Fail / low Pass |
| 2 | Descriptive reflection | "I did Y because I thought it would help." | Pass / low Merit |
| 3 | Dialogic reflection | "I did Y -- but in hindsight Z, and I now see I chose Y partly because it was familiar." | Merit / low Distinction |
| 4 | **Critical reflection** | Level 3 + explicit examination of assumptions, connection to theory or wider context | **Distinction (70+)** |

### 7.2 The depth test

**Level 3 hits ~68. Level 4 hits 72+.** Push every reflective paragraph from Level 3 to Level 4 by adding the **"and I now see this is because..."** move.

Every page should contain at least **two Level-4 moves**.

### 7.3 Ten concrete phrase templates (use literally)

1. **"The uncomfortable realisation was that..."** -- forces Level 4 by naming a belief-revision moment.
2. **"I initially framed this as [X] -- in fact it was [Y], and I now see I preferred the [X] framing because it protected me from [Z]."**
3. **"I told [name] in week [N] that [specific thing]. Their reaction was [honest]. My delivery was [self-critical analysis]. What I would do differently is [concrete mechanism]."** -- THE Insight-72+ feedback template.
4. **"Had I acted on [X] a week earlier, we would have saved [measurable outcome]. The reason I did not was [honest reasoning], a pattern I now recognise in myself."**
5. **"This experience forced me to reframe my belief that [previous]. I now think [new] -- and the evidence that convinced me was [specific incident]."**
6. **"I was wrong about [specific claim]. [Teammate] was right. I dismissed their view in [specific moment] because [honest reason]. The lesson is not that I should always defer -- it is that I should [specific mechanism]."**
7. **"What looked like a technical problem was actually a [communication / trust / incentive] problem, because [mechanism]."**
8. **"The tension between my commitment to [value A] and my commitment to [value B] surfaced in [specific incident]. I had not previously noticed these two values could conflict in me."**
9. **"I will [specific behavioural change] in future team projects, and the way I will know whether it is working is [observable signal]."**
10. **"I leave this project believing that engineering leadership is less about [what I used to think] and more about [what I now think]. The moment this became clear to me was [specific incident]."** -- identity-shift closing.

### 7.4 Opener patterns (Distinction-level)

Do ONE -- never start with "This report will reflect on...":

1. **Drop into a specific scene.** "At 23:47 on the night before the flight demo, I was in the lab deleting my own code."
2. **State the thesis.** "This project taught me that my reflex to solve problems alone is not a strength -- it is a failure mode that only looked like a strength in smaller-scale work."
3. **Name the uncomfortable truth.** "I will argue in this report that the thing I am most proud of is also the thing that most exposed a weakness I had not previously been willing to see."

### 7.5 Closer patterns (Distinction-level)

Do ALL of these:
1. Name what will actually change, with a **mechanism** not a wish.
2. Acknowledge what is still unresolved (intellectual honesty).
3. Connect the personal lesson to professional identity (Move 10).
4. **Avoid triumph.** Distinction closings end with "here is the lesson I will carry", not "and it was a success".

## 8. FAILURE MODES (20)

Every one is a direct cause of dropping from 70s to 60s. Scan and eliminate all.

1. **Chronological trap.** Structuring week-by-week forces description, starves reflection. Structure by theme/lesson.
2. **Generalised teamwork platitudes.** "Communication is key." Automatic Merit ceiling.
3. **Hidden self-praise.** "My main weakness was caring too much about quality." Humblebrag, kills credibility.
4. **Unsupported leadership claims.** "I took a leadership role." Where? When? What changed?
5. **Conflict-free narrative.** No disagreements, no friction. Assume dishonesty or low insight.
6. **Feedback in passive voice.** "Feedback was provided." Who? To whom? Reaction?
7. **Defensive retract.** "I could have done X but the constraints made it unavoidable." Erases the reflection.
8. **Triumphalism.** "The project was a huge success and I am proud." Distinction closings are sober.
9. **Tools-and-tech padding.** "I learned Python, OpenCV, MAVLink." CV bullet, not reflection.
10. **Citing Gibbs/Kolb without using.** Decorative citations with no structural consequence. Worthless.
11. **Vague future commitments.** "I will communicate better." No mechanism = no credit.
12. **Hiding behind the team.** "We decided..." Must be "I" in every reflective paragraph.
13. **Metrics as substitute for reflection.** Progress charts, contribution tables. Not reflection.
14. **Perfect-past syndrome.** Describing past events as if always obvious in the moment.
15. **Feedback-free feedback paragraphs.** Claiming to have given feedback without naming what or how it landed.
16. **Equating "learning about X" with "reflecting on X".** Learning is input. Reflection is what changed.
17. **Gibbs as rigid 6-heading template.** Reproducing all 6 headings screams undergraduate.
18. **Bulleted lists where prose is needed.** Reflection lives in sentences. Lists kill nuance.
19. **Closing with a lessons-learned bullet list.** Project-management artefact, not reflection. Prose only.
20. **Starting with "This report will..."** The most common Merit-ceiling opener. **BANNED.**

## 10. TONE TRAPS

### AI boilerplate detection

Markers are trained to spot AI-generated prose. Red flags:
- Hedged neutral language: "It could be argued that..." / "One might suggest..."
- Perfect parallelism in every paragraph
- Abstract nouns instead of concrete verbs: "facilitation of knowledge transfer" vs "I taught Robin to..."
- Over-hedging: "arguably", "perhaps", "it is worth noting"
- Robotic transitions: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "In addition"

**Fix:** Write in authentic first-person. Short sentences. Specific names, dates, files. Imperfect grammar is fine -- it reads as human.

### Humble brag detection

- "My biggest weakness is that I care too much about quality"
- "I sometimes took on too much because I wanted to help everyone"
- "I could be overly thorough in my documentation"

**Fix:** Name a real weakness that cost the team something. "I delayed the dataset handoff by 3 days because I kept refining instead of releasing a good-enough version."

### Triumphalism detection

- "The project was a resounding success"
- "I am incredibly proud of what we achieved"
- "Our team exceeded all expectations"

**Fix:** Sober closing. "This project confirmed that X; it also exposed Y, which I have not yet fully resolved." Distinction closings are honest about limits.

## 11. PER-PARAGRAPH HEURISTIC (7-point test)

For every paragraph the writer produces, ask:

1. **Is there a specific named incident?** If no -- rewrite.
2. **Is "I" the subject, not "we"?** If no -- rewrite.
3. **Does it revise a belief, name an error, or reframe a prior view?** If no -- Merit ceiling.
4. **Is the self-criticism specific, actionable, proportionate?** If no -- rewrite.
5. **Does it avoid all 20 failure modes (section 8)?** If no -- rewrite.
6. **Is the forward-looking commitment falsifiable?** If no -- add mechanism / observable signal.
7. **Would a cynical marker say "but did this actually change anything?"** If yes -- add evidence of applied change.

**Target:** every page contains 2+ Level-4 moves and zero failure modes.

### Type of self-criticism that is rewarded

- **Specific:** name the behaviour not the trait. ("I dominated the wk3 meeting by interrupting Alex twice" -- NOT "I can be impatient.")
- **Actionable:** paired with a concrete mechanism tested against actual project evidence.
- **Proportionate:** don't flagellate over trivia; don't minimise real mistakes.
- **Forward-looking:** lesson already changed behaviour within the project.
- **Structural, not dispositional:** identify a pattern/system problem, not a personality flaw.

## 17. VOICE & ANTI-AI AUTHENTICITY

This is about making the D7 sound like it was written by a real keen MSc student, not by an AI. The brief says AI usage for reporting is **Category 2: Minimal** — which means AI-generated text is a serious academic integrity risk.

### 17.1 Voice characteristics to HIT (keen MSc student)
- Short sentences mixed with occasional longer ones (not uniform length)
- Occasional imperfect phrasing — real students don't write perfectly polished prose
- Personal quirks: Ukrainian-English patterns (e.g. "I grew up inside" not "I was raised in"), directness, technical specificity over abstraction
- Named specific memories only Apollo would know (commit hashes, 02:14 timestamps, specific teammate reactions)
- Emotional honesty: frustration, pride, confusion, relief — named, not performed
- Self-interruptions: "— actually, no —" or "I want to be honest about this:"
- First-person active verbs: "I wrote", "I cut", "I proposed" (never "it was decided", "one might consider")
- Technical vocabulary used naturally (not explained for a general audience — this IS an engineering MSc)
- References cited as tools used, not decorations: "Drawing on Lencioni's model, which we studied in our Professional Practice sessions, I now recognise..."

### 17.2 AI tells to AVOID (automatic flags)
- Uniform sentence length (AI writes ~20-word sentences consistently)
- Hedging phrases: "it is worth noting", "it could be argued", "this highlights the importance of"
- Paired structures: "not only... but also", "while... it is also true that"
- Smooth transitions: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally" (real students use "Also", "And", "But")
- Perfect parallel structure in lists (real students are messier)
- Abstract nouns: "the importance of communication", "the value of teamwork" (vs "I couldn't get Robin to read my code")
- Concluding summaries that restate the introduction (AI loves circular structure)
- Diplomatic hedging on every criticism (real students are blunter)
- No contractions (real students write "I didn't" not "I did not")
- Overly balanced "on the other hand" reasoning (real students have opinions)
- Generic reflective phrases: "this experience taught me the value of...", "going forward I will strive to..."

### 17.3 Voice scoring rubric
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0-2 | Obviously AI-written. Uniform, hedged, abstract, no personal details |
| 3-4 | Mostly human but with AI tells (smooth transitions, paired structures, generic reflection) |
| 5-6 | Plausibly human. Some personality. Named specifics. But still too polished |
| 7-8 | Convincingly human. Imperfect phrasing, emotional honesty, personal quirks, Ukrainian-English markers |
| 9-10 | Unmistakably Apollo. Could not have been written by anyone else. Specific memories, blunt self-criticism, technical directness, cultural markers |

**Target: 7-8 minimum. Below 5 = academic integrity risk.**

### 17.4 Rewrite rules for humanising AI-drafted text
After any agent writes a section:
1. Replace all "Furthermore/Moreover/Additionally" with "Also/And/But" or delete
2. Add 2-3 contractions per page ("didn't", "couldn't", "won't")
3. Break 1-2 sentences per page into fragments ("Not because I wanted to. Because I had to.")
4. Replace abstract nouns with concrete verbs ("the importance of communication" → "I couldn't get Robin to read my code")
5. Add 1 self-interruption per section ("— actually, that's not quite right —")
6. Vary sentence length: mix 5-word sentences with 30-word ones
7. Remove any sentence that could appear in ANY student's reflection (if it's not specific to Apollo, cut it)
