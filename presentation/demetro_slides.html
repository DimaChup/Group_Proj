SLIDE 1 — VISION (225 words, 1.5 min)
So Edward showed you how we trained the model. Now let me walk you through what actually happens when it runs on the drone. [Point to pipeline] The Raspberry Pi 5 captures frames from a global shutter camera and feeds them into our YOLOv8 model every 200 milliseconds. No GPU, no accelerator — just the Pi's CPU doing inference on each frame. When the model spots a target, it gives us a bounding box in pixels. But pixels don't tell us where to fly. We need GPS coordinates. We know the camera's field of view and the drone's altitude from the barometer, so we project pixel offsets into real-world metres and add them to the drone's GPS position. [Point to tilt comparison] Simple enough — except the drone is never perfectly level. If you ignore that tilt, your estimate lands six metres off. So we read pitch and roll directly from the flight controller and ray-trace through the actual camera angle. That brings the error down to about thirty centimetres. Same principle military surveillance drones use. [Point to four phases] But we don't rely on a single detection. The system refines progressively — detect from altitude, fly toward the estimate, hover directly above, and lock. At hover, the drone's own GPS becomes the target's GPS. Accuracy goes from three and a half metres down to one point eight. The operator sees everything through a browser dashboard and confirms with a single button press. And importantly — the exact same code runs in simulation on a laptop and live on the Pi. One codebase, zero porting.
SLIDE 2 — SIMULATION (222 words, 1.5 min)
[Slide appears, video playing on left] Our testing philosophy is simple: never fly something you haven't proven at every level below it. This ladder shows how we built confidence systematically. [Point to bottom steps] At the base, 127 unit tests validate core logic — GPS math, geofencing, pattern generation. Dry-run mode visualises the full mission without touching hardware. Step two: bench integration on the Pi. Camera, AI model, and flight controller communicating — no propellers, no risk. [Point to middle steps] Over Easter, we went outdoors. Calibrated field-of-view, verified GPS estimation against known positions, confirmed detection at realistic altitudes. Step four: passive flight. A pilot flies manually while the Pi runs detection silently — zero commands sent. Real-world CV validation with zero autonomy risk. [Point to top star] Step five is demo day — full autonomous search, detection, and landing. [Point to video on left] Now, what you're seeing on the left is our simulation running a complete mission. Hardware delays forced us into simulation early — and it became our greatest strength. We verified all twelve requirements: five-layer geofencing with repulsive fields, PLB beacon redirect, payload drop, RTL failsafes, and attitude-compensated GPS estimation — meaning we simulate camera tilt during flight and correct for it so target positions stay accurate even when the drone is banking. Critically, this is one Python codebase throughout. No separate simulation version. What runs in sim is what flies on the Pi. [Strong finish] We're ready. The software is proven. Demo day brings it together. [Hand to Herish]
