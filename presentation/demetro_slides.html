SLIDE 1 — VISION (225 words, 1.5 min)
One of our core requirements was autonomous target geolocation — detect a casualty and return a usable GPS position the drone can fly to. Edward showed you how we trained the model. My job: make that work on a Raspberry Pi with no GPU. [Point to pipeline] The Pi 5 captures frames at 1456 by 1088 from a global shutter camera. Each frame goes through YOLOv8 nano using the NCNN backend — we benchmarked it against TensorFlow Lite and it runs 2.3 times faster: 73 milliseconds versus 200. [Point to bar chart] That's why we chose NCNN — nearly 11 frames per second effective throughput versus under 5 with TFLite. Same accuracy, same weights, just a faster runtime. [Point to pixel→distance diagram] When the model spots a target, we get a bounding box in pixels. We project that pixel offset into real-world metres — the camera's field of view and altitude give us a ground sample distance of 2.2 centimetres per pixel at 35 metres. [Point to tilt comparison] The catch: the drone is never perfectly level. Ignore the tilt and you land six metres off. We read pitch and roll from the flight controller and ray-trace through the actual camera angle. Error drops to thirty centimetres. [Point to bullseye] The bullseye shows it in action — detections from around a known target. Dots coloured red are near the centre of the frame and more accurate; green ones are at the edges. End result: CEP50 of 2.2 metres, on hardware that costs sixty pounds.
SLIDE 2 — SIMULATION (225 words, 1.5 min)
[Slide appears — simulation video plays on the left throughout] The assignment gave us twelve specific requirements the drone must meet. With limited flight time available, we leaned into simulation as the main way to prove our system meets them. [Gesture to video] What you're watching on the left is a full end-to-end mission running in our simulator — takeoff, spiral search, detection, centring on the target, verification, and landing. Same Python codebase that runs on the real Pi. What flies in sim is what flies for real. [Point to Simulation Foundation block] But this isn't just "we ran waypoints in SITL". We replicate the same 1456x1088 camera resolution, simulate camera tilt from pitch and roll, motion blur at speed, GPS drift noise, and synthetic targets composited onto real backgrounds at altitude-correct scale. Every one of the twelve mission requirements is verified end-to-end here — spiral search, detection, geofence, SSSI no-fly zone, PLB redirect, payload drop, RTL failsafes, attitude-compensated GPS, offset landing. [Point to Sim2Real ladder] Simulation alone isn't enough, though — so we built a Sim2Real ladder. Every rung is a real-world check before we trust the system in a full autonomous mission. Unit tests and outdoor calibration on the ground side. Bench integration and passive flight — where a pilot flew while the Pi detected silently, zero commands sent — on the flight side. [Point to unchecked M3, M4] Waypoint flight and auto-detect-and-hover are still to come. Above them: the real mission. [Hand to Herish]
