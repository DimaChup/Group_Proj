\section{My role}

\subsection*{1a. Allocated vs. taken-on roles \rtag{1a}}
\www On paper, at the wk15 kick-off I was allocated \emph{CV / autonomy lead} inside a five-person Company (Robin---flight state machine; [PLACEHOLDER: mechanical lead]---airframe \& payload; Demetro---FDR presentation lead; [PLACEHOLDER: PM]---project management; myself---CV). \textbf{STAR:} \emph{Situation}---by wk16 the promised drone was still unassembled and no aerial training imagery existed. \emph{Task}---keep the software pipeline moving without hardware. \emph{Action}---I built a SITL-backed interactive simulator (\texttt{simple\_simulator.py}, 2508 lines) and a dual-backend vision module (\texttt{vision.py}) so the rest of the team could develop against a stable interface. \emph{Result}---by wk18 the team could run the full mission end-to-end on any laptop with zero drone dependency. Role-drift followed: I took on (implicitly) the roles of simulation maintainer, safety reviewer, and documentation author; I was never voted into them, I was simply the only person the interface forced to own them.
\ebi The correct word for what I did is \emph{owned}, not \emph{led}: I had decision authority on the CV stack but no formal delegation authority over teammates' code. I should have asked the PM in wk17 to re-allocate simulator ownership as a team resource, not a personal project---that would have spread the maintenance load and given me room to coach rather than patch.

\subsection*{1b. What I am proud of \rtag{1b}}
\www \textbf{STAR:} \emph{Situation}---three flight days cancelled, zero seconds of real flight data across eight weeks [\texttt{COMPLAINT\_LETTER.md}]. \emph{Task}---produce verifiable engineering output in spite of a missing platform. \emph{Action}---I wrote \texttt{simple\_simulator.py} (GPS drift, camera shake, CV throttle, pilot UI, 2508 lines), a retrained YOLOv8n model (\texttt{cv\_models/sar\_v2\_1088/}, mAP50 = 0.995 on 366 imgs) and a progressive test ladder (\texttt{tests/flight/0a}$\to$\texttt{4}) covering bench commands, geofence, servo, waypoints and auto-detect. \emph{Result}---simulator-based validation let the team satisfy 12/12 of the R01--R12 brief requirements in simulation by wk22 (commit \texttt{ce5c036}); two external reviewers described the simulator as ``the most complete artefact the Company produced''. This is the single contribution I defend as distinction-level: without it, the project would have had no credible deliverable.
\ebi The same pride is a weakness. I over-invested in a tool I could have simpler; five of the simulator's features (map zoom, Kalman-filtered GPS, spatial clustering, offset-landing math, multi-target prioritisation) were built for a mission I never got to fly. A more disciplined version of me would have shipped a 600-line simulator in wk17 and spent the remaining hours pair-coding with Robin on the mission state machine.

\subsection*{1c. What I would focus on in future \rtag{1c}}
\www I showed I can deliver under constraint, I can diagnose hardware oddities alone (the IMX296 sensor outputting BGR despite being labelled RGB888 took 10+ debugging attempts in one sitting, then a systematic 6-permutation test~\textemdash{} a \emph{Sch{\"o}n reflection-in-action} moment that I now use to teach others not to trust vendor labels), and I can survive the emotional cost of cancelled field days without dropping pace.
\ebi Three things I will deliberately change:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Timebox exploration spikes to 4 hours.} My NCNN backend detour cost two days of calendar time before I abandoned it; a 4-hour spike with a kill-switch criterion would have caught that on the same day. I first applied this rule on 30 Mar and the approach paid off immediately on the geofence-sign investigation.
\item \textbf{Teach early, not late.} I wrote \texttt{docs/VISION\_PIPELINE\_FOR\_COLLEAGUE.md} (2{,}400 words) in wk22 to hand the CV stack to Robbin. If I had written the first half of it in wk18 I would have had a co-owner for the vision code in the last month instead of a reader.
\item \textbf{Refuse silent solo work.} I now flag in stand-ups when I am about to disappear for a long solo session so the team can decide whether it is the right call.
\end{enumerate}
