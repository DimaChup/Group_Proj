# D7_SCORING_FINAL — Delta Summary vs. D7_SCORING_HARSH.md

**New file:** `report/personal_v2/D7_SCORING_FINAL.md` (supersedes `D7_SCORING_HARSH.md`)
**Synthesised from:** `D7_BRIEF_CONSTRAINTS.md`, `D7_AHEP4_GUIDE.md`, `D7_FRAMEWORKS.md`, `D7_EXEMPLARS.md`, and the old HARSH rubric.

## What's new

The old HARSH rubric was strong on hard gates, the verbatim brief, and the three Appendix B descriptors, but it was thin on **how a Distinction-level reflection actually differs from a Merit-level one**. The FINAL rubric keeps everything from HARSH verbatim (brief page 9, Appendix B rubric bands, density/presentation rules, 10-question checklist) and adds four major research-backed layers:

1. **Full AHEP4 M16/M17 sub-clause breakdown** (§4) — treats "Evaluate effectiveness" as the Level 7 step-up and makes the four-element test (criterion + judgement + cause + lesson) mandatory. Adds the Leadership 2/5 threshold test and the Non-technical-audience 2/5 adaptation test — both of which the old rubric missed.
2. **Bristol Level 7 hidden requirements** (§5) — five enforcement rules that are NOT in the brief but are embedded in Bristol's generic Level 7 marking grid: conflict mandatory from 60, leadership mandatory for 70+, media diversity floor caps Comms at 59, peer feedback mandatory for 70+ Insight, and the reflect/evaluate differentiator. These caps were invisible in the old rubric.
3. **The 4-layer framework recommendation** (§6) — WWW/EBI as top-level tag, STAR as per-anecdote micro-structure with mandatory reflection sentence, Kolb ONCE as a ~180-word deep dive with stages named in italics, Schön as a one-sentence label. Replaces the old rubric's vague "use frameworks" guidance with a concrete word budget (2,450 words ≈ 5 pages) and an anecdote inventory mapped to rubric axes.
4. **Hatton & Smith Level 4 depth markers and failure modes** (§9, §10, §13) — the old rubric had a 10-item "failure modes" list; the FINAL version has 20 failure modes from reflection-writing literature, 10 concrete Level-4 phrase templates ("The uncomfortable realisation was that…", "I initially framed this as X but it was Y…"), a 7-point per-paragraph scoring heuristic, and Distinction-level opener/closer patterns.

## What's preserved (unchanged from HARSH)

All hard gates, the verbatim brief extract (Appendix A.2), the verbatim Appendix B rubric descriptors, the 10-question checklist, the density/presentation/readability floors, the staging strategy (7pp working → 5pp final), and the pre-iteration checklist are preserved word-for-word. The old rubric's core was correct — FINAL just layers research-backed depth on top of it.

## Net effect on rewriter/reviewer agents

A new agent reading only `D7_SCORING_FINAL.md` can: (a) score a draft end-to-end via the §12 algorithm including all hidden caps; (b) rewrite a draft using the §6 framework with explicit word budget and §11 anecdote inventory; (c) apply the §13 per-paragraph 7-point test and §14 pre-iteration checklist; (d) quote the verbatim brief and rubric from §2 and §3 without going back to source. The old HARSH rubric capped useful scoring at about 65 because it couldn't distinguish "Merit with polish" from "Distinction with depth"; FINAL explicitly codifies that distinction via Hatton & Smith Level 4 and the AHEP4 evaluate clauses, making 70+ scores actionable rather than aspirational.
