## What Changed Since v4

Four targeted additions (~100 words total), each addressing a specific gap identified in v4's "What keeps it from 85+":

### 1. Root cause of speed-over-inclusion (Section 05)
**Added:** "I suspect this pattern stems from my background: in Ukraine's engineering culture, individual technical competence is prized above collaborative process. Learning to value the slower, more inclusive approach as equally 'productive' is an ongoing adjustment, not a one-time insight."

**Impact:** This directly answers v4's question "WHY didn't you invest more in team development earlier?" The answer -- cultural conditioning toward individual competence -- is specific, honest, and not a generic excuse. "An ongoing adjustment, not a one-time insight" shows the metacognitive awareness that this is a deep pattern, not a checkbox fix. This is exactly the deeper self-interrogation v4 demanded.

### 2. Ground station effectiveness evidence (Section 04)
**Added:** "On the cancelled flight day, the pilot navigated the ground station interface independently during bench testing, confirming targets and reading telemetry without verbal guidance -- the first evidence that the interface worked as intended."

**Impact:** Transforms ground station evaluation from assertion to evidence. The pilot's independent navigation is observable behaviour, not self-reported. "Without verbal guidance" is the key phrase -- it proves the interface design succeeded at its stated goal (non-technical operator usability). This was the single biggest M17 gap in v4.

### 3. Manufacturing lifecycle gap acknowledged (Section 03)
**Added:** "This analysis omits hardware manufacturing and disposal; a full lifecycle assessment would need to account for rare-earth minerals in the Cube flight controller and lithium battery recycling."

**Impact:** Acknowledges the gap rather than ignoring it. This is not a full lifecycle analysis, but it shows the author knows the boundary of their own analysis -- which is itself a form of critical evaluation. Moves M7 from "good but incomplete" to "good and self-aware about its limits."

### 4. Second compromise reflection (Section 04)
**Added:** "The state machine compromise was isolated -- I changed course once in twenty weeks. That ratio suggests my default mode is still autonomous decision-making, and collaborative architecture requires deliberate, sustained effort."

**Impact:** This is cleverer than adding a second compromise example. Instead of fabricating another story, the author reflects on the PATTERN of having only one. "Once in twenty weeks" is brutally honest -- it simultaneously claims the compromise as genuine AND admits its rarity as a weakness. This is higher-order reflection: using the absence of evidence as evidence itself.

## Assessment Summary

### What the v5 additions achieved:

The four targeted additions deliver exactly what was promised: ~100 words, ~+2 points overall. They are efficient and well-placed.

1. **Root cause of speed-over-inclusion** is the highest-value addition. It answers the deepest open question from v4 with a culturally-specific, non-defensive explanation. Worth +1-2 insight points.

2. **Ground station evidence** is the most broadly useful addition, improving both M16 (team development outcome) and M17 (communication effectiveness evaluation). Worth +1-2 across both.

3. **Lifecycle gap acknowledgement** is a small but correct move. One sentence, addresses the M7 weakness cleanly. Worth +1 M7.

4. **"Once in twenty weeks" reflection** is the most intellectually interesting addition. Using absence of evidence as a diagnostic tool is sophisticated. However, its marginal scoring impact is limited because it confirms a weakness rather than demonstrating progress. Worth +0.5 M16 and +1 insight.

### What keeps it at 83-84 (not 85+):

1. **No pattern of bidirectional influence.** The report now honestly admits it has one compromise in twenty weeks. This is excellent self-awareness but it means the M16 evidence base for "flexibility responsive to group members' interests" remains thin. At 85+ the marker would want to see collaborative decision-making as a working mode, not an exceptional event.

2. **Development programme lacks measurement.** Three actions are listed, two have implementation evidence, but none have success metrics. "How will you know this worked?" is unanswered.

3. **No mid-execution crisis.** All failures are retrospective (Pi deployment, camera colour). A story of real-time adaptation under pressure would push self-management higher.

4. **Communication evaluation is still partial.** The ground station now has evidence, but PDR/FDR presentations, documentation effectiveness, and audience adaptation remain evaluated only by assertion.

5. **The report is close to its ceiling without new evidence.** The four additions were the right moves, but they are refinements of existing material. Breaking into 85+ territory would likely require either (a) new evidence from a subsequent project phase (e.g., a successful flight day), or (b) significantly deeper theoretical grounding (e.g., Argyris double-loop learning applied to the speed-over-inclusion pattern).

### Realistic ceiling: 84-85.

The report is now at or near its structural maximum. The writing is clean, the evidence is honest, the reflection is genuine. Further gains require either new experiences to reflect on or deeper theoretical frameworks to analyse existing ones. For a 5-page report based on a cancelled flight day, 83-84 is a strong result.
