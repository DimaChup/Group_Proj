# D7 Reflection Frameworks — Comparison & Recommendation

> Goal: pick the reflection framework(s) that maximise marks on the D7 rubric
> (Teamwork / Self-management / Insight) given the specific constraints:
> 4 top-level headings, 10 mandatory questions, 5-page final limit (7-page working),
> AHEP4 M5/M7/M16/M17 coverage, and an 83+ Insight target that REQUIRES "feedback
> to others to aid their development".

---

## 1. Constraints the framework must satisfy

1. **Page efficiency.** At ~450 words/page, 5 pages = ~2,250 words. With ~10 mandatory
   questions that averages ~200–225 words per reflection. A 6-stage cycle (Gibbs)
   applied literally to each anecdote would blow the budget in one story.
2. **Rubric alignment.** Three criteria, each with an 83+ "differentiator":
   - Teamwork: *responsive to members' interests + creative conflict management*
   - Self-management: *completing ALL tasks across concurrent workstreams*
   - Insight: *feedback to others to aid THEIR development*
3. **Brief compliance.** The D7 brief explicitly names WWW/EBI (p.9). Markers
   anchor on what the brief cites. Using WWW/EBI signals "I read the brief."
4. **Anecdote density.** The report must carry at least: 1 design failure, 2
   conflict examples, 1 feedback-to-others example, 1 full reflective cycle
   walkthrough, + M5/M7/M16/M17 evidence. That is ~6–8 distinct anecdotes.
5. **Active voice + evidence.** "I identified X, I implemented Y, result Z."
   Passive narration ("it was decided that") kills Insight marks.

## 2. Framework-by-framework analysis

### 2.1 Plus / Delta  (brief p.6, PDR context)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure per anecdote | Two columns: what worked (+) / what to change (Δ) |
| Best rubric fit | **Self-management** (good for milestone retrospectives) |
| Conflict narration | Weak — no place for the other party's perspective |
| Feedback-to-others | Poor — inward-facing, about the process not people |
| Verb patterns | Encourages noun phrases ("docs", "stand-ups"), not verbs |
| Page efficiency | Excellent — can be a 2-column mini-table |
| Failure mode | Reads as a checklist, not reflection. Marker sees "bullet point soup" |

**Verdict:** Useful as a *micro-structure* inside a retrospective paragraph
(e.g. "PDR retrospective: + simulation-first saved weeks; Δ I built solo when
I should have paired"). Not strong enough to carry a whole anecdote.

---

### 2.2 What Went Well / Even Better If  (brief p.9, D7 context)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure per anecdote | WWW = strengths evidenced, EBI = self-critique + forward action |
| Best rubric fit | **Insight** (explicitly maps to strengths/weaknesses/self-dev) |
| Conflict narration | Moderate — WWW captures resolution, EBI captures what you'd do next time |
| Feedback-to-others | Moderate — "EBI: I would give Edward earlier structured feedback" |
| Verb patterns | Encourages active voice ("I did X, next time I would do Y") |
| Page efficiency | Very good — 2–3 sentences per slot |
| Failure mode | Can be shallow ("WWW: teamwork. EBI: more teamwork") if not grounded in evidence |

**Verdict:** The brief explicitly names this. It maps almost 1:1 onto the
Insight rubric language ("assess own strengths and weaknesses"). **Strong
top-level candidate.**

---

### 2.3 Gibbs' Reflective Cycle (6 stages)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure | Description → Feelings → Evaluation → Analysis → Conclusion → Action Plan |
| Best rubric fit | **Insight** (feelings + analysis stages give depth) |
| Conflict narration | Excellent — Feelings stage forces you to own emotional content of conflict |
| Feedback-to-others | Moderate — can appear in Action Plan but not built in |
| Verb patterns | Mixed — Description/Feelings tend passive, Analysis/Action active |
| Page efficiency | **Poor.** One full Gibbs cycle ≈ 250–300 words = 0.6 pages. Budget only allows ONE full cycle in the whole report |
| Failure mode | Students over-dwell on Description and Feelings, never reach Analysis |

**Verdict:** Use ONCE as the "deep dive" anecdote (per SCORING_RULES_D7 line 81:
"Walk through one full Kolb/Gibbs cycle properly"). Not a top-level organiser.
The BGR camera failure or the weather-cancelled flight day are the best
candidates for the single Gibbs walkthrough.

---

### 2.4 Schön — Reflection-in-action vs Reflection-on-action

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure | Two modes: thinking while doing vs thinking after doing |
| Best rubric fit | **Self-management** (autonomous problem-solving under time pressure) |
| Conflict narration | Weak — Schön is about individual cognition, not interpersonal |
| Feedback-to-others | Poor |
| Verb patterns | Active — "while debugging I realised...", "afterwards I concluded..." |
| Page efficiency | Excellent as a *lens*, not as a heading structure |
| Failure mode | If cited but not applied, reads as name-dropping |

**Verdict:** Use as a one-sentence *label* on the BGR camera story
("reflection-in-action: I tested all 6 channel permutations rather than trust
the API label"). Signals theoretical literacy for ~1 mark without costing space.
Do NOT use as a section heading.

---

### 2.5 Rolfe — What / So What / Now What

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure | What happened → So what does it mean → Now what do I do |
| Best rubric fit | **Insight** (forces abstraction and forward action) |
| Conflict narration | Good — "So what" forces you to interpret the conflict |
| Feedback-to-others | Moderate — "Now what" can include feedback actions |
| Verb patterns | Active — each stage is a question demanding an answer |
| Page efficiency | **Excellent** — 3 stages × 1 sentence = 60–80 words per anecdote |
| Failure mode | Too terse for deep conflict; can read as bullets |

**Verdict:** The **highest density** framework. Rolfe is effectively the
"compressed Gibbs" — keeps the cycle but drops Description, Feelings, Evaluation,
Conclusion and fuses them into three questions. **Strong candidate as the
per-anecdote micro-structure** inside a WWW/EBI top-level frame.

---

### 2.6 Kolb — Experiential Learning Cycle (4 stages)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure | Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualisation → Active Experimentation |
| Best rubric fit | **Self-management + Insight** (shows a learning programme cycle) |
| Conflict narration | Moderate |
| Feedback-to-others | Poor — individual learning model |
| Verb patterns | Active — especially "Active Experimentation" stage |
| Page efficiency | Medium — 4 stages ≈ 150–200 words |
| Failure mode | Students confuse it with Gibbs; mention it without the Experimentation step |

**Verdict:** Use ONCE, for the "self-development programme" section. The story
of "I built simulator alone → observed team couldn't contribute → generalised
that modular interfaces enable collaboration → experimented with vision.py API
+ web GCS" is a textbook Kolb cycle. Cite by name. One paragraph, ~180 words.

---

### 2.7 STAR — Situation / Task / Action / Result

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure | Context → What you were responsible for → What you did → Outcome |
| Best rubric fit | **Teamwork** (leadership examples with outcomes) + **Self-management** |
| Conflict narration | Excellent — natural fit for "there was tension (S), my role was X (T), I did Y (A), outcome Z (R)" |
| Feedback-to-others | Good — "Action" can be the feedback, "Result" is the teammate's growth |
| Verb patterns | **Strongest active voice of all frameworks.** Demands "I did X" |
| Page efficiency | Good — 4 sentences per anecdote (~80–120 words) |
| Failure mode | Can feel CV-like; doesn't explicitly capture learning/reflection |

**Verdict:** **Best per-anecdote structure for Teamwork and Self-management
evidence.** The Action → Result chain is exactly the "concrete examples with
outcomes" the rubric demands. Weakness: no reflective stage — needs pairing
with WWW/EBI or a "Reflection:" tag after Result.

---

### 2.8 CAR — Context / Action / Result

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Sub-structure | STAR without Task |
| Best rubric fit | Same as STAR but more compressed |
| Conflict narration | Slightly weaker than STAR (Task stage clarifies your role in conflict) |
| Feedback-to-others | Same as STAR |
| Verb patterns | Active |
| Page efficiency | Very good — 3 sentences |
| Failure mode | Loses the "what was expected of me" framing that matters for Teamwork |

**Verdict:** Use only if a STAR anecdote is cramping the budget. STAR's extra
sentence is worth 5–10 words for the clarity it adds on role/responsibility.

## 4. Recommendation — Combined structure for D7

**Do not pick ONE framework. Combine three, each doing what it is best at:**

### Top-level (section headings): WWW/EBI
- The brief explicitly names it on the D7 page (p.9). Using it signals
  compliance and gives the marker the exact vocabulary the rubric uses
  ("strengths and weaknesses").
- Use it as a **tag** on each sub-section, not as the literal section title.
  E.g. "Teamwork and leadership (M16) — What went well, even better if".

### Per-anecdote micro-structure: STAR (+1 reflection sentence)
- Every concrete anecdote (~6–8 in the report) uses a compressed STAR:
  one sentence Situation+Task, one sentence Action, one sentence Result,
  one sentence Reflection (the "EBI" or "next time").
- This forces active voice and an outcome, which is exactly what the rubric
  rewards. Expected length: ~100–120 words per anecdote × 7 = ~800 words = 1.8 pages.
- STAR carries the Teamwork and Self-management evidence. The Reflection
  sentence carries the Insight criterion.

### Deep-dive (ONCE, in the self-development section): Kolb or Gibbs
- Pick ONE story and walk it through all 4 Kolb stages (or all 6 Gibbs).
  SCORING_RULES_D7.md line 81 says this is worth +2 marks.
- Recommended story: **"I built the simulator alone, then generalised from
  the failure"** — textbook Kolb (experience → observation → conceptualisation
  → experimentation via vision.py API + web GCS).
- Budget: ~180 words. Cite Kolb by name. Name each stage in-line in italics.

### Label-only: Schön
- Drop the phrase "reflection-in-action (Schön)" as a one-sentence label on
  the BGR camera debugging story. Costs 4 words, earns ~1 mark for theoretical
  literacy. Do not expand.

```
  Anecdote 2 (STAR): [leadership example with outcome]

  metric. This sentence is the Insight payload.

  Applying Kolb's cycle to this: concrete experience was... reflective
  observation was... abstract conceptualisation was... active experimentation
  was [what I built/changed next]. [~4 sentences, all stages named in italics.]
```

## 6. Failure modes to avoid in the rewrite

1. **Citing frameworks without applying them.** "I used Gibbs' cycle" scores
   zero unless all 6 stages are visible in the text. Either walk it through or
   do not mention it.
2. **Gibbs on every anecdote.** Budget collapses. Gibbs is the ONE deep dive.
3. **STAR without the R.** A lot of the current draft has Situation + Action
   but no measurable Result. Every STAR must end in an outcome.
4. **WWW/EBI with no evidence.** "What went well: teamwork" is a zero-mark
   bullet. Each WWW slot must point at a specific anecdote.
5. **Passive voice in Action.** "It was decided to..." → "I decided to..."
6. **Forgetting the reflection sentence.** STAR alone is CV-writing, not
   reflection. The 4th sentence (EBI/next time) is what converts it to D7.

---

## 7. Final recommendation for the writer agent

> **Use WWW/EBI as the top-level section tag, STAR as the per-anecdote
> micro-structure (with a mandatory reflection sentence after every Result),
> Kolb ONCE for the simulator-solo deep dive, and Schön as a one-sentence
> label on the BGR camera story. Do not use Plus/Delta, Rolfe, or CAR at all —
> they are dominated by the four above.**
>
> Explicit instructions for the rewrite:
> 1. Every anecdote follows: S (1 sentence) → T (1 sentence) → A (1–2
>    sentences, active voice, "I…") → R (1 sentence with a metric or
>    observable outcome) → Reflection (1 sentence starting "Even better if"
>    or "Next time").
> 2. One section (the self-development section) contains a ~180-word paragraph
>    explicitly walking through Kolb's four stages with each stage named in
>    italics: *concrete experience*, *reflective observation*, *abstract
>    conceptualisation*, *active experimentation*.
> 3. The BGR camera anecdote carries the parenthetical "(reflection-in-action,
>    Schön)" once and no more.
> 4. Every section heading is followed immediately by two sub-tags in italics:
>    *What went well.* and *Even better if.* (exactly as in the brief p.9).
> 5. Budget: ~9 STAR anecdotes × ~100 words + 1 Kolb paragraph ≈ 1,080 words
>    of anecdote. Reserve ~1,170 words for AHEP4 framing, M5/M7 content, intro,
>    and conclusion. Total ~2,250 words ≈ 5 pages.
> 6. The Insight-83+ anecdote (feedback to Edward / FIELD_QUICK_REF / GitHub
>    workflow mentoring) MUST be present and MUST end with an observable
>    outcome for the OTHER person ("Edward shipped his first detection PR",
>    "three teammates ran tests independently after the quick-ref").
