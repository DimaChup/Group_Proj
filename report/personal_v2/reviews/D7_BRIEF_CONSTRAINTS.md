### Length requirements

- **Maximum length: 5 pages** — p9: "Maximum length: 5 pages, excluding cover page and appendices."
- **Excluded from 5-page count**: cover page, appendices — p9 (same quote).
- **Main body = 5 pages hard cap.** Going over risks penalty; stopping well below risks appearing under-engaged.

### Structural requirements (MUST answer these specific questions)

p9 states: **"Answer the questions below, reflecting on your specific experiences, within your specific team."** The four sections and sub-questions are MANDATORY structural anchors:

1. **My role**
   a. "What were your roles and responsibilities in this project? Which of these were explicitly allocated, and which did you take on/were you given implicitly? Did they change over the course of the project?"
   b. "What elements of your contribution are you most happy about and/or proud of?"
   c. "What different things might you focus on in your future work?"

2. **My team**
   a. "How was your team structured? Was this discussed explicitly? What changed over the course of the project?"
   b. "What elements of team working do you think were most impactful on your outputs?"
   c. "What would you change if you were to do a similar project in future?"

3. **My impact**
   a. "How did your contributions drive the team forward and contribute to success?"
   b. "What might you add or re-focus on in future projects?"

4. **My support**
   a. "What did other team members do that helped you work more effectively?"
   b. "What actions did you take that helped other individuals and/or the team as a whole work more effectively?"

Omitting any of these four sections or their sub-questions is a direct rubric hit.

### Content requirements

- **Focus on self, not team achievements** — p9: "the focus of this reflection is on what you did, but what you do differently in comparable future situations." Also: "is on what you did and how you developed, and what you might do differently."
- **Constructive reflection** — p9: "A useful framework here is to encourage constructive reflection so is to consider 'what went well', and 'even better if'."
- **Influence on working practices/outputs** — p9: "You may include anecdotes from team activities, but the focus of this reflection is on what you did to influence the team's working practices and outputs."
- **Personal development narrative** — p5: "D7 Individual Reflective Report ... Summary of contribution and personal development."
- **AHEP4 M16 (teamwork/individual effectiveness)** — p9: "M16. Function effectively as an individual, and as a member or leader of a team. Evaluate effectiveness of own and team performance." Must evidence BOTH self-evaluation AND team evaluation.
- **AHEP4 M17 (communication)** — p9: "M17. Communicate effectively on complex engineering matters with technical and non-technical audiences, evaluating the effectiveness of the methods used." Must evidence communication reflection with evaluation of methods.

### Assessment / rubric requirements (Appendix B, p12)

Marked against three criteria at Level 7 university marking criteria:

1. **Teamwork** (bands: 0/7/15/22/29/35 → 42/45/48 → 52/55/58 → 62/65/68 → 72/75/78 → 83/94/100)
2. **Self-management**
3. **Insight**

To hit the TOP band (83/94/100) the brief requires:

- **Teamwork top band**: "Shows outstanding ability to work and lead a team with creativity and flexibility that is responsive to group members' interests and the obligations and goals of the team. Able to manage conflict."
- **Self-management top band**: "Works autonomously demonstrating outstanding self-organisational skills and behaviours. Has a professional attitude to completing all tasks."
- **Insight top band**: "Shows confidence in working autonomously and setting own goals. Can assess own strengths and weaknesses. Able to identify and implement an effective programme of self-development to improve practical and professional skills. Can provide effective feedback to others and their self-development."

To hit the 72–78 band (realistic strong target):

- **Teamwork**: "Consistently demonstrates effective teamworking and leadership skills and ability to work effectively to meet their obligations and goals. Able to manage conflict."
- **Self-management**: "Works autonomously demonstrating very good self-organisational skills and behaviours. Has a professional attitude to completing tasks."
- **Insight**: "Demonstrates ability to work autonomously and assess own strengths and weaknesses. Demonstrates ability to identify and implement an effective programme of self-development to improve practical and professional skills."

### Required demonstrations (implicit from rubric wording)

- **Conflict management evidence** — "Able to manage conflict" appears from band 62 upward. Missing = cap at ~58.
- **Autonomy evidence** — "Works autonomously" appears from 62 upward in both Self-management and Insight.
- **Self-assessment of strengths AND weaknesses** — appears from band 42 upward in Insight.
- **Self-development programme** — "identify and implement an effective programme of self-development" is required from band 62 upward.
- **Proactive approach** — band 62 (52/55/58) Insight: "Has a professional attitude to self-development and professional skills."
- **Practical AND professional skills** — Insight rubric differentiates both at top bands.

## NOT ALLOWED / DISCOURAGED (what to avoid)

### AI usage restrictions

- **AI Category 2: Minimal for reporting and presentation** — p5: "AI usage is permitted per guidelines at https://www.bristol.ac.uk/bilt/sharing-practice/guides/guidance-on-ai-using-ai-in-assessment/, with restrictions on: o Software development – Category 3: Selective o Reporting and presentation – Category 2: Minimal."
- Category 2 Minimal means: AI may assist with limited, clearly-acknowledged uses (e.g. proofreading, brainstorming) but cannot generate content that is presented as the student's own reflection. A reflective report that reads as AI-generated risks academic misconduct.
- **Reflective content specifically must be the student's own voice** — implicit: a reflection the author didn't actually experience cannot be outsourced to AI by definition. Personal experience is unforgeable.

### Academic integrity

- **All submissions will be checked for academic integrity** — p5: "Submissions will be checked for academic integrity and are subject to University regulations, information at https://www.bristol.ac.uk/digital-education/assessment-online/academic-integrity/."
- **University regulations apply** — same quote.
- **Unverifiable claims risk misconduct flag** — implicit from p2 footnote 1: "Coordinates are available in AENGM0074.kml. These will not be visibly marked in any field trials, but teams should report any excursions/incursions verbally during trials and in post-flight reports, and flight logs may be examined to verify compliance." Brief sets a precedent that claims can and will be cross-checked.

### Out of scope for D7

- **Team achievements as the main subject** — p9: "the focus is on what you did and how you developed" — the team's technical wins belong in D6, not D7.
- **Technical designs, architectures, schematics** — these belong in D6 (p8: "System description The architecture of your final system. Flow charts, schematics, images recommended.").
- **Requirements verification, evaluation, STEEPLE** — these are D6 sections (p8).
- **Gantt chart, work packages** — these belong in D6 mandatory appendix (p8: "Mandatory appendix (assessed) Project management documentation, including Gantt chart, work packages/deliverables, risk matrix.").
- **Duplication of D6 content** — implicit: D7 is the PERSONAL complement to the GROUP D6. Rehashing group-report content wastes the 5-page budget.

### Stylistic / rhetorical avoidance (implicit from Level 7 marking criteria)

- **Passive voice and generic claims** — Level 7 marking criteria differentiate "description" (low band) from "evaluation" (high band). See p12 Insight row: low bands say "Displays limited awareness" / "Shows some ability to identify"; top bands say "Shows confidence" / "Can provide effective feedback." Descriptive writing caps at ~45–55.
- **Unsupported claims / unverifiable stories** — p5 integrity clause means any anecdote must be genuine.
- **Hedged prose** — Rubric wants "confidence" (top band Insight) and "consistently demonstrates" (top band Teamwork). Tentative language ("I think maybe I sort of contributed") maps onto low-band descriptors.
- **Pure description** — top band Insight requires "identify and implement an effective programme of self-development," which is action, not narration.

### Page/format discipline

- **Exceeding 5 pages** — p9 hard cap. Late-penalty equivalent treatment assumed.
- **Omitting cover page** — p9 excludes cover page from count, implying it is expected.
- **Submitting in non-PDF** — p9 explicit: "single PDF".

## OPTIONAL (may or may not help)

- **References / citations** — brief does NOT explicitly require references in D7 (unlike D6 p8 which lists "References" as a mandatory section). For D7, references are optional. Citing Professional Practice concepts (p9) earns implicit credit but is not mandatory.
- **Figures / diagrams** — brief does NOT mention figures for D7. D6 recommends "Flow charts, schematics, images recommended" (p8) — D7 has no equivalent statement. Figures in D7 appendices are allowed; figures eating the 5-page budget are risky.
- **Appendices beyond main 5 pages** — p9 excludes appendices from page count. No cap on how many. But unlike D6, D7 has no explicit mandatory appendix. Pure optional.
- **Formal reflective framework references** (Gibbs, Schön, Kolb, Plus/Delta) — p9 only explicitly names "Plus/Delta review" (in D1 context, p6) and "what went well / even better if." Using Gibbs or Schön is permitted and signals wider reading but not required.
- **Appendix evidence** (commit logs, photos, Git contributions graphs, meeting notes, self-development reading list) — strongly implied as legitimate evidence sources by the rubric's demand for substantiated autonomy and self-development claims, even though not explicitly listed for D7.
- **Named incidents** (specific meeting, specific bug, specific conflict) — p9: "free to discuss specifics" permits but does not require.

## IMPLICIT (not stated but expected at Level 7)

- **Professional writing quality** — MSc Level 7 baseline. Typos, grammatical errors, poor structure cap marks regardless of content.
- **Originality of thought** — p5 Level 7 marking criteria is referenced throughout; the word "originality" appears in D6 rubric top band ("demonstrating originality"). For D7, originality manifests as genuine self-insight, not stock phrases.
- **Self-critical honesty** — "even better if" framing (p9) explicitly asks for weaknesses. Airbrushed reports cap in the middle bands.
- **Evidence of autonomous learning** — Rubric Self-management top bands say "Works autonomously" twice. Autonomy must be shown, not asserted.
- **Evaluation vs description** (Level 7 differentiator) — The leap from mid-band to top-band in every rubric row is "aware" → "able to" → "demonstrates" → "outstanding". Each step requires evaluative judgement (not narrative retelling).
- **Closing the reflective loop** — Brief's sub-questions repeatedly ask "what would you change / focus on in future?" — this is Kolb-style experiential learning. Missing future-oriented content caps the Insight score.
- **Responsiveness to team context** — Teamwork top band: "responsive to group members' interests." Writing about the team's goals without engaging with individuals' goals misses this descriptor.
- **Feedback skill** — Insight top band: "Can provide effective feedback to others and their self-development." Writing only about receiving, not giving, feedback caps Insight at ~78.
- **Conflict evidence** — "Able to manage conflict" threshold at 62+. Projects without any mentioned conflict raise a flag: either it existed and was hidden (dishonest) or the author wasn't close enough to the team to see it (low engagement).
- **Balance across the 4 sections** — Implicit equal weighting: My role, My team, My impact, My support. Heavily skewing toward one section (e.g. all "My role" and no "My support") signals imbalance.

## SUMMARY: WHAT A TOP-BAND D7 LOOKS LIKE (derived from brief only)

1. PDF, 5 pages main body, cover page, optional appendices.
2. Four top-level sections literally titled "My role", "My team", "My impact", "My support" (or close variants).
3. Every sub-question in p9 answered with at least one concrete, verifiable specific.
4. At least one named conflict and how it was managed.
5. Honest self-assessment including weaknesses, not just strengths.
6. A named self-development programme (books, skills, courses) with actions, not plans.
7. Evidence of autonomous goal-setting ("I set X goal, I hit it by Y date").
8. At least one instance of giving feedback/coaching to a teammate.
9. Engagement with Professional Practice unit concepts (named framework).
10. Forward-looking statements in every section (future projects).
11. Level-7 evaluative voice — "I evaluated", "this demonstrated", "in hindsight this was effective because" — not descriptive narrative.
12. AHEP4 M16 and M17 implicitly covered: effectiveness evaluation of self/team (M16) and communication methods (M17).
13. Weaponised rubric language: "autonomously", "effective programme of self-development", "professional attitude", "manage conflict", "responsive to", "practical and professional skills".
14. Appendix evidence (commits, artefacts, reading list, meeting notes) supporting every non-trivial claim.
15. No D6 duplication, no AI-generated fluff, no passive hedging.
