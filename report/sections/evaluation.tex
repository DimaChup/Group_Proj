\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

The plus/delta review below assesses technical performance against the 12 requirements (R01--R12) using four evidence sources: SITL simulation (Tier~1, 100+ hours), Docker environment tests (Tier~2), bench tests on the assembled Pi~5 hardware (Tier~3, one field day), and DJI flight video replayed through the detection pipeline (Tier~3 proxy). No outdoor flight took place (Tiers~4--5 outstanding). D7 addresses team-working aspects individually.

\textbf{Architecture resilience (P1, P2, P5, P8).}
The single-interface vision module (\texttt{detect\_in\_image}) enabled three model retrains (640, 1280, 1088\,px datasets) with zero changes to the orchestrator or ground station. Adding lens undistortion---not anticipated during initial design---required modifying one file and added only \SI{1.5}{\milli\second} per frame.

\textbf{Detection performance (P4, D2, D4, D5, D9).}
The retrained YOLOv8n achieved mAP$_{50}$ = 0.995 (validation set) and 50/50 bench detection at 0.966 mean confidence (Figure~\ref{fig:conf_vs_alt}). At \SI{35}{\metre}, the target subtends 36 model-input pixels---a comfortable margin above the 20\,px floor. However, the 0.995 mAP$_{50}$ likely overestimates real-world accuracy due to train/validation overlap (only 16 of 366 images are from real footage). The confidence threshold (0.2) was set by visual inspection rather than a precision--recall curve sweep; characterising this trade-off is a priority for future work. FP16 deployment could double throughput to ${\sim}$\SI{10}{FPS} (Figure~\ref{fig:latency_breakdown}).

\textbf{GPS estimation accuracy (D3).}
The pipeline projects each detection's pixel offset through the calibrated camera model and the drone's reported position to produce a GPS estimate. DJI flight video analysis with known ground-truth positions yielded CEP$_{50}$ = \SI{2.3}{\metre} and a worst-case error of \SI{16.5}{\metre} (Figures~\ref{fig:gps_bullseye} and~\ref{fig:gps_error_direction}). The worst case stems from GPS timing lag compounding with heading-aligned motion. Inverse-variance-weighted averaging and Kalman filtering partially mitigate this bias, but first-order lag compensation (offsetting GPS by $v \cdot \Delta t_{\text{lag}}$ in the heading direction) remains unimplemented.

\textbf{Testing effectiveness (P3, P7).}
The progressive framework caught 12 defects at Tiers~1--3, each resolved in minutes to hours at zero hardware risk: 5 at Tier~1 (simulation, 1.5\,hr total), 6 at Tier~2 (Pi bench, 4.7\,hr), and 1 at Tier~3 proxy. Five would have caused mission-critical failures in flight (geofence sign inversion, repulsive force reversal, barometric drift loop, duplicate target queuing, and missing timeout guard), yet all were trivially reproducible in simulation. Appendix~\ref{app:bug-cost} provides the full defect register with resolution times and assessed flight-day impact.

\textbf{Weather cancellation as information-maximisation, not failure (D1).}
The scheduled outdoor flight was cancelled due to sustained winds of \SI{12}{\metre\per\second} with gusts to \SI{18}{\metre\per\second}---above the EDU-450's \SI{10}{\metre\per\second} operational limit. This cancellation represents the single largest gap in the project, eliminating any possibility of Tier~4/5 validation. However, the team treated the bench day as an opportunity for information maximisation rather than a binary failure, executing four activities that would not have occurred had the flight proceeded on schedule:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{FOV calibration} ($f{=}7.0 \to 5.46$\,mm, tape measure at 1\,m) --- corrected a \SI{28}{\percent} GPS estimation bias that would have produced landing errors exceeding the \SI{10}{\metre} R07 radius.
    \item \textbf{Lens distortion mapping} (RMS = 0.399, 14$\times$9 checkerboard) --- confirmed that the IMX296 global shutter has negligible barrel distortion and undistortion is optional.
    \item \textbf{Inference benchmarking} (50 runs $\times$ 3 models) --- established the \SI{206.5}{\milli\second} baseline and validated 50/50 detection at 0.966 mean confidence.
    \item \textbf{DJI video analysis pipeline} (built post-cancellation) --- yielded the confidence-vs-altitude curve (Figure~\ref{fig:conf_vs_alt}), detection-vs-speed analysis (Figure~\ref{fig:det_vs_speed}), and GPS accuracy characterisation (CEP$_{50}{=}\SI{2.3}{\metre}$, Figure~\ref{fig:gps_bullseye}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent These four data products form the quantitative backbone of this evaluation. The project lacks \emph{system-level} flight validation but possesses \emph{subsystem-level} quantitative data exceeding what a single flight sortie would typically yield (Appendix~\ref{sec:field-day}).

\textbf{Incomplete flight validation (D1, D6, D7, D8).}
The integrated system has never executed a search pattern over real terrain. Detection range versus altitude in natural lighting, false-positive rate against real backgrounds, wind-induced motion blur, and GPS accuracy under dynamic manoeuvres remain unvalidated (only DJI video proxy data are available). Bench results provide reasonable confidence that outdoor operation will require parameter tuning rather than architectural changes.

\subsection{Testing Framework Summary}\label{sec:test-framework-summary}

The five-tier progressive testing framework (Table~\ref{tab:five-tier-summary}) advanced from pure software to full autonomy, with each tier serving as a gate that must pass before the next is attempted. This structure caught 12 defects at low-cost tiers (Appendix~\ref{app:bug-cost}) and is the primary reason no architectural faults remained by the time hardware was assembled.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{Five-tier progressive testing framework (condensed from Appendix~\ref{sec:five-tier}).}\label{tab:five-tier-summary}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clp{7.5cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Tier} & \textbf{Environment} & \textbf{What It Verifies} \\
\midrule
1 & SITL simulation & State machine logic, path planning, geofence, detection pipeline---zero hardware risk. \\
2 & Docker Pi test & TFLite loads on ARM, dependency compatibility, headless operation. \\
3 & Bench (Pi + Cube, no props) & MAVLink ACKs, camera frames, GPS fix, full sensing chain. \\
4 & Passive flight (pilot on RC) & Real-world detection range, FOV/GPS calibration---Pi issues zero commands. \\
5 & Autonomous flight & End-to-end mission: search, detect, centre, verify, land. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Tiers~1--3 were fully exercised; Tier~4 was partially achieved through DJI video replay as a proxy after weather cancellation. Tier~5 remains outstanding. Of the 12 defects (Appendix~\ref{app:bug-cost}), 5 were caught at Tier~1 (simulation, \SI{1.5}{\hour} total fix time), 6 at Tier~2 (Pi bench, \SI{4.7}{\hour}), and 1 at Tier~3 proxy (video analysis, mitigation pending). Two intermediate tiers unique to this project---Docker environment testing (Tier~2) and passive zero-command flight (Tier~4)---are absent from most university drone projects yet caught six of the twelve defects. Appendix~E (\secref{sec:five-tier}) details the full five-tier methodology and per-tier script inventory.

\paragraph{Summary.}
Tiers~1--3 produced strong quantitative baselines: 12 defects caught and resolved in ${\sim}\SI{6}{\hour}$\,\SI{10}{\minute} total, \SI{206.5}{\milli\second} inference confirmed over 50 runs, CEP$_{50}{=}\SI{2.3}{\metre}$ GPS accuracy from video, and mAP$_{50}{=}0.995$ (upper bound due to train/val overlap). However, the system remains \emph{incompletely validated}: Tiers~4--5 (passive and autonomous outdoor flight) were never executed, so requirements R05, R06, and R07 are verified only in simulation.

Of the nine delta items, three are parameter-tuning gaps (D2, D3, D4) resolvable without code changes, two are functional gaps requiring implementation (D6, D8), one is a field-testing gap (D7), and three are methodological limitations (D1, D5, D9) requiring additional flight time and data. The progressive testing framework retired the highest-cost integration faults in ${\sim}\SI{6}{\hour}$ of bench time; completing the remaining tiers requires scheduling, favourable weather, and resolution of D6/D8. Appendix~\ref{app:lessons-learned} details lessons learned and second-iteration improvements.
