An autonomous search and rescue (SAR) drone was designed, implemented, and tested by a team of five MSc students for the AENGM0074 group project. The system locates a casualty surrogate within the designated search area at Fenswood Farm, subject to geofencing constraints including a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) no-fly zone, a 50\,m altitude ceiling, and defined flight boundaries. All twelve brief requirements (R01--R12) are addressed---verified through simulation, bench testing, and field-day calibration, with outdoor flight validation pending---via a \textbf{four-layer automatic geofence} (SSSI exclusion, flight-area boundary, altitude ceiling, repulsive buffer), operator-in-the-loop verification, and a public MIT-licensed GitHub repository.

\paragraph{Mission objective.}
The drone autonomously takes off, searches a polygon-defined area using a lawnmower pattern, detects a casualty via onboard computer vision, reports the target's GPS coordinates and imagery to a ground operator for confirmation, commands a landing within 10\,m of the casualty to deploy a first-aid kit, and returns to the take-off location. An operator-in-the-loop verification stage prevents autonomous action on false positives.

\paragraph{System overview.}
The platform is a Hexsoon EDU-450 hexacopter controlled by a Cube Orange+ autopilot running ArduPilot. A Raspberry Pi~5 companion computer runs all onboard intelligence: a YOLOv8n object-detection model exported to TensorFlow Lite (TFLite), a lawnmower search-pattern generator, GPS-based target localisation with inverse-variance-weighted spatial clustering, and a finite state machine with \textbf{20~states} and 32~transitions that orchestrates the complete mission. A browser-based ground station streams live Motion JPEG (MJPEG) video and provides per-target classification controls (confirm, reject, mark as interest, mark as false positive) over Wi-Fi. Four MCDA trade studies informed the selection of detection model, search pattern, companion computer, and ground-station architecture.

\paragraph{Key design decisions.}
Three architectural choices define the system. First, a \emph{modular single-codebase} design isolates computer vision, path planning, flight control, and configuration into independent modules with narrow interfaces, allowing identical code to run across laptop simulation, bench hardware, and the Pi without modification. Second, a \emph{dual-backend vision pipeline} automatically selects between Ultralytics/PyTorch on the development laptop and TFLite on the Pi, enabling rapid iteration without deployment friction. Third, a \emph{simulation-first development methodology} validated each subsystem through a \textbf{five-tier progressive testing framework}---SITL simulation, hardware component checks, bench integration, passive field observation, and autonomous flight---with \textbf{58~test scripts} organised across six categories, catching interface and timing errors before integration.

\paragraph{Results achieved.}
The detection model, retrained on 366 mixed synthetic and real-world images at native camera resolution ($1456 \times 1088$), achieves \textbf{mAP$_{50} = 0.995$}. On-device inference runs at \textbf{206\,ms} per frame (\textbf{4.8\,FPS}) on the Pi~5 CPU via the XNNPACK delegate, sufficient for the altitude-dependent search speed (8\,m/s at the nominal 35\,m altitude). Lens distortion correction adds only 1.5\,ms overhead. Post-hoc analysis of DJI flight video over the test site confirmed detection across a 15--50\,m altitude range and GPS target localisation with a Circular Error Probable (CEP) of \textbf{CEP$_{50} = 2.3$\,m}. The lawnmower pattern covers the survey polygon in approximately 1.1\,minutes with 18~waypoints, verified via dry-run to avoid SSSI encroachment. All figures in this report are generated programmatically from calibration data and benchmarks, ensuring reproducibility.

\paragraph{Field day and weather adaptation.}
Full hardware integration---Pi, Cube, camera, and GPS---was validated on the assembled airframe during a scheduled field day at Fenswood Farm. Adverse weather (sustained high winds exceeding safe flight limits) prevented powered flight; the team pivoted to an information-maximising bench-testing protocol on-site, producing lens calibration data (RMS\,=\,0.399), FOV characterisation (5.46\,mm focal length), and inference benchmarks confirming 100\,\% detection at 0.966 mean confidence across 50~consecutive frames. These calibration results were fed directly back into the vision pipeline, improving GPS estimation accuracy for subsequent flights. In parallel, the complete mission pipeline---arming, search, detection, operator verification, and landing---was exercised end-to-end in Software-In-The-Loop (SITL) simulation, and DJI flight video recorded over the test site served as a real-world proxy for altitude-dependent detection analysis. No autonomous outdoor flight was achieved; all integration results are from simulation, bench tests, and DJI video proxy analysis. The progressive testing methodology, operator-in-the-loop architecture, and four-layer automatic geofence provide a validated foundation for autonomous SAR operation with consumer-grade hardware and open-source software once outdoor flight conditions permit.
