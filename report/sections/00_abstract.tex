Search and rescue (SAR) operations require rapid coverage of large areas, yet ground-based human search teams are constrained by terrain, fatigue, and limited field of view, resulting in slow response times during the critical early hours of an incident \cite{goodrich2008}. This paper presents the design, implementation, and validation of a low-cost autonomous SAR drone capable of detecting casualties using onboard computer vision without reliance on cloud connectivity.

The system employs a Raspberry Pi 5 running a YOLOv8n object detection model \cite{yolov8} exported to TensorFlow Lite \cite{tflite}, achieving real-time inference at 206\,ms per frame (4.8\,FPS). A finite state machine orchestrates the mission: autonomous takeoff, lawnmower search pattern over a defined polygon, AI-based casualty detection, GPS-referenced target localisation, controlled descent for visual confirmation, and precision landing. An operator-in-the-loop verification stage ensures that no autonomous action is taken on false positives.

The detection model, retrained on 366 mixed synthetic and real-world images at native camera resolution ($1456 \times 1088$), achieves a mean average precision (mAP50) of 0.995. A progressive testing methodology---from software-in-the-loop simulation through bench testing to field trials---validates each subsystem independently before integration. The complete compute platform costs under \$500, demonstrating that effective autonomous SAR capability is achievable with consumer-grade hardware and open-source software.
