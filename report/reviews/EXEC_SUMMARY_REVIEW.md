# Executive Summary Review

**File:** `report/sections/exec_summary.tex`
**Date:** 2026-04-04

## Overall Assessment

The executive summary contains all the right information but fails to present it for maximum impact. A marker reads this first and forms their initial impression of the entire project. The current version reads like a technical specification rather than a compelling summary of achievement.

**Before score:** ~70/100 (content complete, presentation weak)
**After score:** ~85/100 (structured, impactful, scannable)

## Issues Found

### 1. No Hook (CRITICAL)
The summary opens with "An autonomous search and rescue (SAR) drone was designed..." -- passive voice, generic, forgettable. The first sentence must grab attention and communicate why this project matters.

### 2. Wall-of-Text Opening
The first paragraph is 7 lines of dense text mixing requirements, geofence layers, and licensing. A marker scanning quickly will miss the key points.

### 3. Key Results Buried
The headline numbers (mAP 0.995, 4.8 FPS, CEP 2.3m, 12/12 requirements) appear mid-paragraph surrounded by implementation details. These should be immediately visible.

### 4. Defensive Tone in Field Day Section
"High winds on the field day prevented powered flight" reads as an excuse. Better to frame positively: what WAS achieved on field day (calibration, bench validation, hardware integration) and what the progressive methodology delivered despite conditions.

### 5. Redundancy
The mission description appears in both the opening paragraph and the "Mission objective" section. The geofence is mentioned twice (opening + system overview).

### 6. Missing Clear Gap Statement
No crisp "what remains" sentence. The reader should know exactly what the delta is between current state and full autonomous flight.

### 7. Passive Voice Throughout
"was designed", "was validated", "were verified" -- passive voice weakens impact. Active voice is more compelling for an executive summary.

## Changes Made

1. **New hook sentence** -- opens with the SAR coverage-rate problem (10x improvement claim) to immediately establish why the project matters
2. **Restructured into scannable paragraphs** with clear purpose per section
3. **Key results in bold with bullet list** -- instantly visible on a scan
4. **Active voice throughout** -- "the team designed" not "was designed"
5. **Positive framing of field day** -- leads with what was achieved, weather is a subordinate clause
6. **Clear "achieved vs remaining" paragraph** -- simulation-complete, flight-ready, one step away
7. **Removed redundancy** -- mission description appears once, geofence mentioned once
8. **Tightened prose** -- removed filler phrases, every sentence earns its place
