## CRITICAL Issues (must fix before submission)

### 1. Equation 2 double-counts U-turn hover energy

**Location:** Lines 62--65, Eq. (2)

The equation as written is:

```
E_total = P_hover * t_total  +  P_drag(v) * t_flight  +  n_turns * dt_turn * P_hover
```

The text defines `t_total` as "total airborne time (including turns)". Since `t_total` already includes U-turn time, the `P_hover * t_total` term already accounts for hover power during U-turns. The third term `n_turns * dt_turn * P_hover` then adds it **a second time**.

**The script (`optimize_path.py`, lines 387--390) does NOT double-count** -- it correctly computes `E = P_hover * t_total + P_drag * t_flight` where `t_total` includes U-turn penalty time and `t_flight` excludes it. The table values are generated by the script and are therefore correct. Only the LaTeX equation is wrong.

**Fix:** Remove the third term from Eq. (2) and clarify the decomposition:

```latex
E_{\mathrm{total}} = \underbrace{P_{\mathrm{hover}} \cdot t_{\mathrm{total}}}_{\text{hover (incl.\ turns)}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{P_{\mathrm{drag}}(v) \cdot t_{\mathrm{flight}}}_{\text{drag (forward motion only)}}
```

where `t_total = t_flight + n_turns * dt_turn`.

### 2. Motion blur critical speed claim is wrong at low altitudes

**Location:** Lines 296--297

The text states: "The critical speed for single-pixel blur exceeds 30 m/s at **all altitudes**."

This is only true at 50 m. The critical speed is `GSD / exposure_time`:

| Altitude | GSD (m/px) | Critical speed (m/s) |
|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| 20 m     | 0.0126    | **12.6**            |
| 25 m     | 0.0158    | 15.8                |
| 30 m     | 0.0189    | 18.9                |
| 35 m     | 0.0221    | 22.1                |
| 40 m     | 0.0253    | 25.3                |
| 50 m     | 0.0316    | 31.6                |

At 20 m, single-pixel blur occurs at 12.6 m/s -- above the 6 m/s search speed, so blur is still sub-pixel in practice (0.48 px). But the claim "exceeds 30 m/s at all altitudes" is factually incorrect.

**Fix:** Change to: "The critical speed for single-pixel blur exceeds the search speed at every altitude in the operating range (12.6 m/s at 20 m vs 6 m/s search speed; 31.6 m/s at 50 m vs 10 m/s search speed)."

## HIGH Issues (should fix)

### 4. Detection probability table: model pixel sizes use incorrect YOLO scaling

**Location:** Lines 281--292, Table (tab:detect-chain), "Model (px)" column

The table applies **separate** scale factors for tall (640/1088 = 0.588) and wide (640/1456 = 0.440) dimensions. YOLOv8 uses **letterboxing** which preserves the aspect ratio: the single scale factor is `min(640/1456, 640/1088) = 640/1456 = 0.4396` for both dimensions.

| Alt | Paper "Model (px)" | Correct letterbox (px) |
|-----|-------------------|----------------------|
| 20 m | 84 x 18 | **63 x 17** |
| 25 m | 67 x 14 | **50 x 14** |
| 30 m | 56 x 12 | **42 x 12** |
| 35 m | 48 x 10 | **36 x 10** |
| 40 m | 42 x 9  | **31 x 9**  |
| 50 m | 34 x 7  | **25 x 7**  |

The tall dimension is overstated by ~33%. The wide dimension is correct. This matters for the argument at line 302 about the "48 px" target at 35 m and the "10 px comfort threshold" -- with correct scaling, the target is 36 px tall and 10 px wide, which still clears the threshold but with less margin than claimed.

Similarly, the claim at line 336 that the mannequin width at 50 m is "7 pixels -- below the 10 pixel comfort threshold for width" remains valid (7 px is correct), but the tall value at 50 m should be 25 px, not 34 px.

**Impact on P_d values:** The P_d column likely remains valid because it was benchmarked empirically (per-frame p values) rather than computed from pixel size alone. But the intermediate "Model (px)" column should be corrected.

### 5. Confidence threshold inconsistency

**Location:** Line 300

The text says "a single detection above the **0.2** confidence threshold triggers investigation." The actual code uses **0.4** (set in `vision.py`, confirmed in CLAUDE.md). This is a factor-of-2 discrepancy.

**Fix:** Either update the text to 0.4, or note that 0.2 was used for the detection probability analysis while 0.4 is the operational threshold.

### 6. Flight time ceiling: "approximately 18 minutes" is imprecise

**Location:** Line 21

At the selected operating point (278 W), the flight time is 115.4 / 278 * 60 = **24.9 min**. At 50 m / 10 m/s (350 W), it's **19.8 min**. Even with 80% usable capacity (92.3 Wh), it's 19.9 min at 278 W. The "18 min" figure doesn't match any obvious scenario.

**Fix:** Either compute at a specific power level and state the assumption, or say "approximately 20 minutes" which is closer to reality at both power levels.

## MEDIUM Issues (recommended fixes)

### 7. P_eff formula is conceptually muddled

**Location:** Lines 347--349

```
P_eff = C * P_d + (1 - C) * P_access * P_d ≈ 0.96 * 0.9997 ≈ 0.96
```

This formula implies detection can occur in the uncovered area (the `P_d` term in the second addend), which contradicts the premise. The intent is to say the uncovered 4% has negligible prior probability of containing a casualty. The correct formulation:

```
P_eff = P(target in covered area) * P_d + P(target in uncovered area) * 0
      = C * P_d ≈ 0.96 * 0.9997 ≈ 0.96
```

The numerical result (0.96) is unchanged regardless of formulation. The issue is purely formal -- an examiner may flag the formula as nonsensical.

### 8. Swath spacing formula not explicitly defined

**Location:** Lines 31, 80--81

The text defines ground footprint width `W_g = w_s * h / f` but never explicitly defines the swath spacing formula. The script uses `swath = ground_h * (1 - overlap)` where `ground_h = W_g * (H_px / W_px)` -- i.e., the **shorter** image dimension drives the swath spacing. The `path_strategies.md` reference document uses the **wider** dimension (`25.7 m` at 35 m = `W_g * 0.8`), which is inconsistent with the optimization script.

The tex should explicitly state which image dimension corresponds to the cross-track direction (perpendicular to scan line) and derive the swath spacing from that.

### 9. Pareto table: "Ideal" NFZ margin column is misleading

**Location:** Lines 317--331, Table (tab:pareto-final)

The table lists NFZ margin "Ideal = 50 m, Selected = 50 m" with "34 m surplus above footprint radius." The 50 m figure combines the 30 m waypoint buffer and 20 m slow zone, but these zones serve different purposes. The actual camera-edge-to-SSSI clearance is 30 - 16.1 = **13.9 m** at 35 m altitude. The 50 m figure overstates the physical clearance.

## Positive Assessment

### What works well

1. **Energy model is physically sound.** The hover + quadratic drag decomposition is standard and the parameter values (150 W hover, 50 W drag at 5 m/s) are plausible for a 2.3 kg quadcopter. The script implementation is correct.

2. **216-configuration sweep is well-executed.** 6 altitudes x 36 angles is a thorough exploration. The energy heatmap and altitude-energy plots are genuinely informative. The valley at 70 deg and the altitude dependence are clear.

3. **Strategy comparison matches `path_strategies.md` exactly.** All six strategies, path lengths, times, energies, and qualitative assessments are consistent between the tex and the reference document. The percentage claims check out: creeping line is 75% more energy (14.5/8.3 = 1.75x), expanding square is 23% more (within the 18-23% range), spiral is 11.9% longer path (within 8-12%).

4. **Detection probability chain is complete.** The GSD -> pixel extent -> model input -> dwell time -> frame count -> P_d chain is traced end-to-end with correct physics (except the YOLO scaling issue in item 4).

5. **All four figures exist in `report/figs/`.** `real_energy_heatmap.png`, `real_energy_vs_altitude.png`, `real_path_patterns.png`, and `real_optimal_pattern.png` are all present and referenced correctly.

6. **NFZ interaction analysis is thorough.** The three-layer defense (waypoint exclusion, speed ramp, hard boundary), the per-segment numerical integration, and the geometric insight about angle alignment vs NFZ exposure are all well-argued.

7. **Selected configuration justification is strong.** The argument for 35 m over 50 m (pixel margin, model robustness, acceptable energy cost, rescan safety net) is well-structured and avoids the trap of simply picking the energy minimum.

8. **Cross-references to other sections** (focus area, NFZ avoidance, rescan chain) are present and the section is self-contained enough to stand alone.
